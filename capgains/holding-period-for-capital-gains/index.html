India Capital Gains Tax

Holding period for capital gains

A capital asset is short-term if you held it for not more than 24 months before the transfer; for a security listed on a recognised stock exchange in India, a unit of the Unit Trust of India, a unit of an equity-oriented fund or a zero-coupon bond the limit is 12 months (section 2 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, read on 13 November 2026). A capital asset that is not short-term is long-term.

AssetLong-term if held more than
Listed shares (any listed security)12 months
Equity-oriented fund unit, UTI unit, zero-coupon bond12 months
Unlisted shares, land, building, gold and everything else24 months

How the period is counted

The section adds in the period for which the previous owner held the asset in the cases it lists (for example an asset that comes to you under section 73(1), shares received in an amalgamation or demerger), and leaves out the period after a company goes into liquidation for shares in it.

Common questions

How many months make a gain long-term?

More than 24 months for most assets, and more than 12 months for a security listed on a recognised stock exchange in India, a unit of the Unit Trust of India, a unit of an equity-oriented fund or a zero-coupon bond (section 2 of the Income-tax Act, 2025).
