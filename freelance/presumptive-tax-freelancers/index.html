Presumptive taxation for freelancers: section 58

Section 58 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 lets small taxpayers declare a fixed share of their receipts as profit instead of keeping full books. The Table in section 58(2) has the cases that matter to freelancers (read on 13 November 2026).

Serial 3: a specified profession (the old section 44ADA)

  • Who: an individual or a firm other than an LLP, resident in India, carrying on a specified profession (the list).
  • Limit: gross receipts not above ₹50 lakh, or not above ₹75 lakh where cash receipts are not more than 5% of gross receipts.
  • Income: 50% of gross receipts, or the profit actually earned if higher.

Serial 1: any other business (the old section 44AD)

  • Who: an eligible assessee: a resident individual, HUF or firm other than an LLP that has not claimed certain deductions, does not carry on a specified profession, earns no commission or brokerage and runs no agency business.
  • Limit: turnover not above ₹2 crore, or ₹3 crore where cash receipts are not above 5%.
  • Income: 6% of the part received by a specified banking or online mode (in the tax year or before the return due date) plus 8% of the rest, or the profit actually earned if higher.

What the scheme does and does not allow

  • No loss, allowance or deduction is allowed against the deemed income (section 58(4)); the written down value of assets is worked as if depreciation had been allowed (section 58(6)).
  • If you declare less than the deemed profit and your income is above the exemption limit, you keep books (section 62) and get an audit (section 63) (section 58(3)).
  • A cheque or bank draft that is not account payee counts as cash (section 58(9)).

Dates

An assessee under serial 1 or 3 pays the whole advance tax by 15 March (section 408(2)). The return is due on 31 July after the tax year for an assessee who is not a company, not audited and not in the 30 November group (section 263(1)(c)).

Common questions

What happens if I leave the 6%/8% scheme?

Section 58(7): an eligible assessee under Table serial 1 who declares profit lower than the scheme for any of the five years after a year in which the scheme was used loses the benefit for five tax years, and must keep books and get an audit if income exceeds the exemption limit.

Does a cheque count as cash?

A cheque or bank draft that is not account payee counts as cash for the 5% test (section 58(9)).
