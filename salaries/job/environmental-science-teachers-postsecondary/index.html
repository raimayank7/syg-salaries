Environmental Science Teachers, Postsecondary: pay in the United States, May 2025

Environmental Science Teachers, Postsecondary in the United States earned a median wage of $94,980 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Teach courses in environmental science. Includes both teachers primarily engaged in teaching and those who do a combination of teaching and research. Excludes “Forestry and Conservation Science Teachers, Postsecondary” (25-1043).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-1053.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Environmental Science Teachers, Postsecondary is $94,980, which is 86% higher. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 15 by median pay. With 6,690 jobs, it makes up about 0.4 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Environmental Science Teachers, Postsecondary pay by state

The highest median pay was in Kentucky ($139,110), Maryland ($131,060) and Oklahoma ($113,680). The lowest was in Arkansas ($59,910), Alabama ($64,700) and Mississippi ($76,210). The most jobs were in New York (610), Texas (380) and New Jersey (350). The occupation is most concentrated in Vermont, where its share of all jobs is 7.4 times the national share.
