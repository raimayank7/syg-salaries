Nursing Instructors and Teachers, Postsecondary: pay in the United States, May 2025

Nursing Instructors and Teachers, Postsecondary in the United States earned a median wage of $80,250 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Demonstrate and teach patient care in classroom and clinical units to nursing students. Includes both teachers primarily engaged in teaching and those who do a combination of teaching and research.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-1072.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Nursing Instructors and Teachers, Postsecondary is $80,250, which is 57% higher. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 24 by median pay. With 77,960 jobs, it makes up about 5.0 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Nursing Instructors and Teachers, Postsecondary pay by state

The highest median pay was in Hawaii ($129,530), District of Columbia ($107,770) and Delaware ($98,170). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($58,210), West Virginia ($62,210) and Kansas ($62,870). The most jobs were in Texas (6,830), New York (6,340) and California (5,610). The occupation is most concentrated in Hawaii, where its share of all jobs is 6.0 times the national share.
