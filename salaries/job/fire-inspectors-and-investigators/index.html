Fire Inspectors and Investigators: pay in the United States, May 2025

Fire Inspectors and Investigators in the United States earned a median wage of $75,920 a year ($36.50 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Inspect buildings to detect fire hazards and enforce local ordinances and state laws, or investigate and gather facts to determine cause of fires and explosions.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 33-2021.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Fire Inspectors and Investigators is $75,920, which is 49% higher. Among the 24 occupations of the field Protective Service, it ranks number 8 by median pay. With 13,800 jobs, it makes up about 0.9 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Fire Inspectors and Investigators pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($122,900), Oregon ($115,080) and California ($99,230). The lowest was in Oklahoma ($37,320), West Virginia ($52,490) and Mississippi ($55,260). The most jobs were in California (1,350), New Jersey (1,290) and New York (1,200). The occupation is most concentrated in New Jersey, where its share of all jobs is 3.4 times the national share.
