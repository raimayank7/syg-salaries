Biological Technicians: pay in the United States, May 2025

Biological Technicians in the United States earned a median wage of $57,510 a year ($27.65 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Assist biological and medical scientists. Set up, operate, and maintain laboratory instruments and equipment, monitor experiments, collect data and samples, make observations, and calculate and record results. May analyze organic substances, such as blood, food, and drugs.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 19-4021.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Biological Technicians is $57,510, which is 13% higher. Among the 48 occupations of the field Life, Physical, and Social Science, it ranks number 43 by median pay. With 69,620 jobs, it makes up about 4.5 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Biological Technicians pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($71,440), New Jersey ($66,040) and District of Columbia ($64,770). The lowest was in South Dakota ($41,300), Idaho ($42,130) and Guam ($44,950). The most jobs were in California (9,210), Massachusetts (8,430) and New York (5,050). The occupation is most concentrated in Massachusetts, where its share of all jobs is 5.2 times the national share.
