Exercise Physiologists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Exercise Physiologists in the United States earned a median wage of $59,460 a year ($28.59 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Assess, plan, or implement fitness programs that include exercise or physical activities such as those designed to improve cardiorespiratory function, body composition, muscular strength, muscular endurance, or flexibility. Excludes “Physical Therapists” (29-1123), “Athletic Trainers” (29-9091), and “Exercise Trainers and Group Fitness Instructors” (39-9031).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-1128.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Exercise Physiologists is $59,460, which is 17% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 62 by median pay. With 8,560 jobs, it makes up about 0.6 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Exercise Physiologists pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($81,310), Oregon ($71,630) and Minnesota ($67,230). The lowest was in Mississippi ($43,060), Utah ($43,850) and South Carolina ($46,550). The most jobs were in California (640), Pennsylvania (640) and Michigan (570). The occupation is most concentrated in Delaware, where its share of all jobs is 3.5 times the national share.
