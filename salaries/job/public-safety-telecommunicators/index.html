Public Safety Telecommunicators: pay in the United States, May 2025

Public Safety Telecommunicators in the United States earned a median wage of $53,040 a year ($25.50 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Operate telephone, radio, or other communication systems to receive and communicate requests for emergency assistance at 9-1-1 public safety answering points and emergency operations centers. Take information from the public and other sources regarding crimes, threats, disturbances, acts of terrorism, fires, medical emergencies, and other public safety matters. May coordinate and provide information to law enforcement and emergency response personnel. May access sensitive databases and other information sources as needed. May provide additional instructions to callers based on knowledge of and certification in law enforcement, fire, or emergency medical procedures.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 43-5031.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Public Safety Telecommunicators is $53,040, which is 4% higher. Among the 54 occupations of the field Office and Administrative Support, it ranks number 14 by median pay. With 102,500 jobs, it makes up about 6.6 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Public Safety Telecommunicators pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($80,120), Washington ($78,850) and Oregon ($71,980). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($26,620), Mississippi ($34,510) and Arkansas ($38,240). The most jobs were in California (8,520), Texas (7,730) and Florida (6,090). The occupation is most concentrated in West Virginia, where its share of all jobs is 1.9 times the national share.
