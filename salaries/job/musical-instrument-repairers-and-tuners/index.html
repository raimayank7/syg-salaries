Musical Instrument Repairers and Tuners: pay in the United States, May 2025

Musical Instrument Repairers and Tuners in the United States earned a median wage of $46,420 a year ($22.32 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Repair percussion, stringed, reed, or wind instruments. May specialize in one area, such as piano tuning. Excludes “Audiovisual Equipment Installers and Repairers" (49-2097) who repair electrical and electronic musical instruments.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 49-9063.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Musical Instrument Repairers and Tuners is $46,420, which is 9% lower. Among the 51 occupations of the field Installation, Maintenance, and Repair, it ranks number 47 by median pay. With 5,380 jobs, it makes up about 0.3 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Musical Instrument Repairers and Tuners pay by state

The highest median pay was in Michigan ($59,570), New Jersey ($58,730) and Tennessee ($58,730). The lowest was in Mississippi ($23,980), Georgia ($29,640) and Oklahoma ($31,160). The most jobs were in Texas (580), California (520) and Tennessee (300). The occupation is most concentrated in Maine, where its share of all jobs is 3.9 times the national share.
