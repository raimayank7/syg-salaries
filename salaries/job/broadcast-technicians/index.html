Broadcast Technicians: pay in the United States, May 2025

Broadcast Technicians in the United States earned a median wage of $59,570 a year ($28.64 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Set up, operate, and maintain the electronic equipment used to acquire, edit, and transmit audio and video for radio or television programs. Control and adjust incoming and outgoing broadcast signals to regulate sound volume, signal strength, and signal clarity. Operate satellite, microwave, or other transmitter equipment to broadcast radio or television programs.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 27-4012.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Broadcast Technicians is $59,570, which is 17% higher. Among the 41 occupations of the field Arts, Design, Entertainment, Sports, and Media, it ranks number 28 by median pay. With 21,110 jobs, it makes up about 1.4 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($67,960) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Broadcast Technicians pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($108,270), New Hampshire ($81,150) and Pennsylvania ($80,470). The lowest was in West Virginia ($27,310), Mississippi ($27,750) and Utah ($31,490). The most jobs were in California (2,340), New York (1,690) and Texas (1,380). The occupation is most concentrated in District of Columbia, where its share of all jobs is 3.1 times the national share.
