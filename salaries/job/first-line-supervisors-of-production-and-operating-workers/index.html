First-Line Supervisors of Production and Operating Workers: pay in the United States, May 2025

First-Line Supervisors of Production and Operating Workers in the United States earned a median wage of $74,450 a year ($35.79 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Directly supervise and coordinate the activities of production and operating workers, such as inspectors, precision workers, machine setters and operators, assemblers, fabricators, and plant and system operators. Excludes team or work leaders.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 51-1011.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for First-Line Supervisors of Production and Operating Workers is $74,450, which is 46% higher. Among the 105 occupations of the field Production, it ranks number 8 by median pay. With 673,430 jobs, it makes up about 43 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

First-Line Supervisors of Production and Operating Workers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Wyoming ($90,390), Connecticut ($83,150) and Rhode Island ($81,650). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($45,100), Virgin Islands ($57,190) and Guam ($57,440). The most jobs were in Texas (58,930), California (48,550) and Ohio (31,980). The occupation is most concentrated in Alabama, where its share of all jobs is 2.3 times the national share.
