Loading and Moving Machine Operators, Underground Mining: pay in the United States, May 2025

Loading and Moving Machine Operators, Underground Mining in the United States earned a median wage of $74,500 a year ($35.82 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Operate underground loading or moving machine to load or move coal, ore, or rock using shuttle or mine car or conveyors. Equipment may include power shovels, hoisting engines equipped with cable-drawn scraper or scoop, or machines equipped with gathering arms and conveyor.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 47-5044.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Loading and Moving Machine Operators, Underground Mining is $74,500, which is 46% higher. Among the 60 occupations of the field Construction and Extraction, it ranks number 7 by median pay. With 5,930 jobs, it makes up about 0.4 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Loading and Moving Machine Operators, Underground Mining pay by state

The highest median pay was in Wyoming ($112,110), Alaska ($93,130) and Nevada ($82,920). The lowest was in Oklahoma ($44,760), Texas ($47,310) and Iowa ($48,490). The most jobs were in West Virginia (1,250), Nevada (880) and Alabama (510). The occupation is most concentrated in West Virginia, where its share of all jobs is 46.6 times the national share.
