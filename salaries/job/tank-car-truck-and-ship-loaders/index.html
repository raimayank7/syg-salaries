Tank Car, Truck, and Ship Loaders: pay in the United States, May 2025

Tank Car, Truck, and Ship Loaders in the United States earned a median wage of $58,870 a year ($28.30 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Load and unload chemicals and bulk solids, such as coal, sand, and grain, into or from tank cars, trucks, or ships, using material moving equipment. May perform a variety of other tasks relating to shipment of products. May gauge or sample shipping tanks and test them for leaks.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 53-7121.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Tank Car, Truck, and Ship Loaders is $58,870, which is 15% higher. Among the 50 occupations of the field Transportation and Material Moving, it ranks number 20 by median pay. With 10,700 jobs, it makes up about 0.7 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Tank Car, Truck, and Ship Loaders pay by state

The highest median pay was in Maryland ($81,320), South Carolina ($80,700) and Montana ($80,070). The lowest was in Arkansas ($33,800), Oklahoma ($35,360) and Virginia ($41,190). The most jobs were in Texas (1,620), Ohio (820) and Louisiana (500). The occupation is most concentrated in North Dakota, where its share of all jobs is 10.3 times the national share.
