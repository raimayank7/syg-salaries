General and Operations Managers: pay in the United States, May 2025

General and Operations Managers in the United States earned a median wage of $105,770 a year ($50.85 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Plan, direct, or coordinate the operations of public or private sector organizations, overseeing multiple departments or locations. Duties and responsibilities include formulating policies, managing daily operations, and planning the use of materials and human resources, but are too diverse and general in nature to be classified in any one functional area of management or administration, such as personnel, purchasing, or administrative services. Usually manage through subordinate supervisors. Excludes First-Line Supervisors.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 11-1021.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for General and Operations Managers is $105,770, which is 107% higher. Among the 37 occupations of the field Management, it ranks number 23 by median pay. With 3,503,020 jobs, it makes up about 225 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($134,940) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

General and Operations Managers pay by state

The highest median pay was in New Jersey ($173,690), District of Columbia ($168,000) and South Dakota ($137,400). The lowest was in Guam ($67,690), Puerto Rico ($73,570) and Arkansas ($74,050). The most jobs were in Texas (431,310), California (300,840) and Florida (236,290). The occupation is most concentrated in District of Columbia, where its share of all jobs is 2.3 times the national share.
