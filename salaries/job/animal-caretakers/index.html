Animal Caretakers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Animal Caretakers in the United States earned a median wage of $35,360 a year ($17.00 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Feed, water, groom, bathe, exercise, or otherwise provide care to promote and maintain the well-being of pets and other animals that are not raised for consumption, such as dogs, cats, race horses, ornamental fish or birds, zoo animals, and mice. Work in settings such as kennels, animal shelters, zoos, circuses, and aquariums. May keep records of feedings, treatments, and animals received or discharged. May clean, disinfect, and repair cages, pens, or fish tanks. Excludes “Veterinary Assistants and Laboratory Animal Caretakers” (31-9096) and “Farmworkers, Farm, Ranch, and Aquacultural Animals” (45-2093).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 39-2021.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Animal Caretakers is $35,360, which is 31% lower. Among the 32 occupations of the field Personal Care and Service, it ranks number 25 by median pay. With 266,910 jobs, it makes up about 17 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Animal Caretakers pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($44,210), Washington ($40,570) and Hawaii ($40,130). The lowest was in Mississippi ($26,970), Puerto Rico ($27,600) and Louisiana ($27,910). The most jobs were in California (25,710), Texas (20,450) and Florida (17,210). The occupation is most concentrated in Kentucky, where its share of all jobs is 1.8 times the national share.
