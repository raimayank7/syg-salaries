Light Truck Drivers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Light Truck Drivers in the United States earned a median wage of $44,860 a year ($21.57 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Drive a light vehicle, such as a truck or van, with a capacity of less than 26,001 pounds Gross Vehicle Weight (GVW), primarily to pick up merchandise or packages from a distribution center and deliver. May load and unload vehicle. Excludes “Couriers and Messengers” (43-5021) and “Driver/Sales Workers” (53-3031).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 53-3033.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Light Truck Drivers is $44,860, which is 12% lower. Among the 50 occupations of the field Transportation and Material Moving, it ranks number 34 by median pay. With 983,300 jobs, it makes up about 63 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Light Truck Drivers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Alaska ($57,190), District of Columbia ($49,330) and Washington ($49,070). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($24,430), Guam ($25,100) and Virgin Islands ($31,180). The most jobs were in California (111,950), Texas (78,540) and Florida (67,390). The occupation is most concentrated in South Dakota, where its share of all jobs is 1.7 times the national share.
