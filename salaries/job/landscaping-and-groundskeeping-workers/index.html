Landscaping and Groundskeeping Workers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Landscaping and Groundskeeping Workers in the United States earned a median wage of $39,150 a year ($18.82 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Landscape or maintain grounds of property using hand or power tools or equipment. Workers typically perform a variety of tasks, which may include any combination of the following: sod laying, mowing, trimming, planting, watering, fertilizing, digging, raking, sprinkler installation, and installation of mortarless segmental concrete masonry wall units. Excludes “Farmworkers and Laborers, Crop, Nursery, and Greenhouse” (45-2092).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 37-3011.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Landscaping and Groundskeeping Workers is $39,150, which is 23% lower. Among the 10 occupations of the field Building and Grounds Cleaning and Maintenance, it ranks number 8 by median pay. With 952,640 jobs, it makes up about 61 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Landscaping and Groundskeeping Workers pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($48,790), Massachusetts ($48,670) and Alaska ($47,690). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($21,910), Guam ($31,620) and Mississippi ($31,630). The most jobs were in California (106,180), Florida (86,040) and Texas (63,430). The occupation is most concentrated in Hawaii, where its share of all jobs is 1.8 times the national share.
