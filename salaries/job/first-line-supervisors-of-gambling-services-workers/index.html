First-Line Supervisors of Gambling Services Workers: pay in the United States, May 2025

First-Line Supervisors of Gambling Services Workers in the United States earned a median wage of $63,820 a year ($30.68 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Directly supervise and coordinate activities of workers in assigned gambling areas. May circulate among tables, observe operations, and ensure that stations and games are covered for each shift. May verify and pay off jackpots. May reset slot machines after payoffs and make repairs or adjustments to slot machines or recommend removal of slot machines for repair. May plan and organize activities and services for guests in hotels/casinos.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 39-1013.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for First-Line Supervisors of Gambling Services Workers is $63,820, which is 25% higher. Among the 32 occupations of the field Personal Care and Service, it ranks number 2 by median pay. With 26,010 jobs, it makes up about 1.7 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

First-Line Supervisors of Gambling Services Workers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($77,350), Oregon ($76,960) and Alaska ($76,340). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($37,180), Nebraska ($43,610) and South Dakota ($47,720). The most jobs were in Nevada (5,590), California (3,010) and Oklahoma (1,160). The occupation is most concentrated in Nevada, where its share of all jobs is 21.5 times the national share.
