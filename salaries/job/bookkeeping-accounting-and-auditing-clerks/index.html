Bookkeeping, Accounting, and Auditing Clerks: pay in the United States, May 2025

Bookkeeping, Accounting, and Auditing Clerks in the United States earned a median wage of $50,670 a year ($24.36 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Compute, classify, and record numerical data to keep financial records complete. Perform any combination of routine calculating, posting, and verifying duties to obtain primary financial data for use in maintaining accounting records. May also check the accuracy of figures, calculations, and postings pertaining to business transactions recorded by other workers. Excludes “Payroll and Timekeeping Clerks” (43-3051).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 43-3031.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Bookkeeping, Accounting, and Auditing Clerks is $50,670, which is about the same. Among the 54 occupations of the field Office and Administrative Support, it ranks number 17 by median pay. With 1,373,680 jobs, it makes up about 88 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Bookkeeping, Accounting, and Auditing Clerks pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($65,180), Connecticut ($59,970) and Massachusetts ($59,830). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($28,700), Guam ($33,280) and Mississippi ($42,840). The most jobs were in California (151,870), Texas (122,400) and Florida (88,670). The occupation is most concentrated in South Dakota, where its share of all jobs is 2.5 times the national share.
