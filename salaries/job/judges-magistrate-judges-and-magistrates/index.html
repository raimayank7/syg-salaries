Judges, Magistrate Judges, and Magistrates: pay in the United States, May 2025

Judges, Magistrate Judges, and Magistrates in the United States earned a median wage of $153,990 a year ($74.03 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Arbitrate, advise, adjudicate, or administer justice in a court of law. May sentence defendant in criminal cases according to government statutes or sentencing guidelines. May determine liability of defendant in civil cases. May perform wedding ceremonies.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 23-1023.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Judges, Magistrate Judges, and Magistrates is $153,990, which is 202% higher. Among the 8 occupations of the field Legal, it ranks number 2 by median pay. With 24,030 jobs, it makes up about 1.5 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Judges, Magistrate Judges, and Magistrates pay by state

The highest median pay was in Rhode Island ($239,760), Massachusetts ($221,710) and Connecticut ($217,760). The lowest was in Mississippi ($49,960), Michigan ($51,210) and Alabama ($52,850). The most jobs were in Texas (2,770), New York (2,270) and Ohio (1,720). The occupation is most concentrated in Puerto Rico, where its share of all jobs is 3.4 times the national share.
