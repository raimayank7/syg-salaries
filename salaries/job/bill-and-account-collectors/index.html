Bill and Account Collectors: pay in the United States, May 2025

Bill and Account Collectors in the United States earned a median wage of $47,030 a year ($22.61 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Locate and notify customers of delinquent accounts by mail, telephone, or personal visit to solicit payment. Duties include receiving payment and posting amount to customer’s account, preparing statements to credit department if customer fails to respond, initiating repossession proceedings or service disconnection, and keeping records of collection and status of accounts.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 43-3011.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Bill and Account Collectors is $47,030, which is 8% lower. Among the 54 occupations of the field Office and Administrative Support, it ranks number 32 by median pay. With 158,830 jobs, it makes up about 10 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Bill and Account Collectors pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($62,230), Alaska ($60,810) and Massachusetts ($59,800). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($25,570), Guam ($37,320) and Mississippi ($37,680). The most jobs were in Texas (21,720), California (13,880) and Florida (13,180). The occupation is most concentrated in Puerto Rico, where its share of all jobs is 3.0 times the national share.
