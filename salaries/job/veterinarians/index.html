Veterinarians: pay in the United States, May 2025

Veterinarians in the United States earned a median wage of $130,100 a year ($62.55 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Diagnose, treat, or research diseases and injuries of animals. Includes veterinarians who conduct research and development, inspect livestock, or care for pets and companion animals.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-1131.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Veterinarians is $130,100, which is 155% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 31 by median pay. With 83,900 jobs, it makes up about 5.4 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Veterinarians pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($163,920), Maryland ($163,170) and Washington ($160,510). The lowest was in South Dakota ($88,650), Puerto Rico ($89,810) and Wyoming ($97,240). The most jobs were in California (9,170), Texas (6,270) and Florida (5,360). The occupation is most concentrated in Montana, where its share of all jobs is 1.8 times the national share.
