Nuclear Medicine Technologists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Nuclear Medicine Technologists in the United States earned a median wage of $101,370 a year ($48.74 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Prepare, administer, and measure radioactive isotopes in therapeutic, diagnostic, and tracer studies using a variety of radioisotope equipment. Prepare stock solutions of radioactive materials and calculate doses to be administered by radiologists. Subject patients to radiation. Execute blood volume, red cell survival, and fat absorption studies following standard laboratory techniques.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-2033.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Nuclear Medicine Technologists is $101,370, which is 99% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 35 by median pay. With 17,080 jobs, it makes up about 1.1 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Nuclear Medicine Technologists pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($162,380), Washington ($126,200) and Hawaii ($125,650). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($52,930), Kansas ($64,610) and West Virginia ($76,460). The most jobs were in Florida (1,740), California (1,500) and Texas (1,060). The occupation is most concentrated in South Dakota, where its share of all jobs is 2.8 times the national share.
