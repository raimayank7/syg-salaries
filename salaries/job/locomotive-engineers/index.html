Locomotive Engineers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Locomotive Engineers in the United States earned a median wage of $81,410 a year ($39.14 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Drive electric, diesel-electric, steam, or gas-turbine-electric locomotives to transport passengers or freight. Interpret train orders, electronic or manual signals, and railroad rules and regulations. Excludes “Engineers” (17-2011 through 17-2199).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 53-4011.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Locomotive Engineers is $81,410, which is 60% higher. Among the 50 occupations of the field Transportation and Material Moving, it ranks number 8 by median pay. With 33,470 jobs, it makes up about 2.2 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Locomotive Engineers pay by state

The highest median pay was in New York ($109,220), Massachusetts ($108,790) and Mississippi ($96,710). The lowest was in North Carolina ($61,190), Ohio ($63,280) and Louisiana ($64,200). The most jobs were in Texas (3,780), Illinois (2,340) and California (1,570). The occupation is most concentrated in Montana, where its share of all jobs is 4.3 times the national share.
