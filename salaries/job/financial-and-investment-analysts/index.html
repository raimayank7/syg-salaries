Financial and Investment Analysts: pay in the United States, May 2025

Financial and Investment Analysts in the United States earned a median wage of $102,740 a year ($49.40 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Conduct quantitative analyses of information involving investment programs or financial data of public or private institutions, including valuation of businesses. Excludes “Budget Analysts” (13-2031), “Financial Risk Specialists” (13-2054), and “Securities, Commodities, and Financial Services Sales Agents” (41-3031).

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Financial and Investment Analysts is $102,740, which is 102% higher. Among the 32 occupations of the field Business and Financial Operations, it ranks number 3 by median pay. With 361,980 jobs, it makes up about 23 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($116,800) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Financial and Investment Analysts pay by state

The highest median pay was in Wyoming ($187,090), New York ($127,930) and Oregon ($120,590). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($63,000), West Virginia ($69,030) and Virgin Islands ($69,990). The most jobs were in New York (52,380), California (45,380) and Texas (28,820). The occupation is most concentrated in New York, where its share of all jobs is 2.3 times the national share.
