Materials Engineers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Materials Engineers in the United States earned a median wage of $112,860 a year ($54.26 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Evaluate materials and develop machinery and processes to manufacture materials for use in products that must meet specialized design and performance specifications. Develop new uses for known materials. Includes those engineers working with composite materials or specializing in one type of material, such as graphite, metal and metal alloys, ceramics and glass, plastics and polymers, and naturally occurring materials. Includes metallurgists and metallurgical engineers, ceramic engineers, and welding engineers.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 17-2131.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Materials Engineers is $112,860, which is 121% higher. Among the 36 occupations of the field Architecture and Engineering, it ranks number 10 by median pay. With 22,770 jobs, it makes up about 1.5 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Materials Engineers pay by state

The highest median pay was in New Mexico ($165,600), Maryland ($140,640) and Washington ($139,840). The lowest was in Iowa ($64,270), Montana ($76,030) and Mississippi ($78,700). The most jobs were in California (3,020), Ohio (1,790) and Texas (1,440). The occupation is most concentrated in New Mexico, where its share of all jobs is 3.6 times the national share.
