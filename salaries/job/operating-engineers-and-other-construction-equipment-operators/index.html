Operating Engineers and Other Construction Equipment Operators: pay in the United States, May 2025

Operating Engineers and Other Construction Equipment Operators in the United States earned a median wage of $59,850 a year ($28.78 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Operate one or several types of power construction equipment, such as motor graders, bulldozers, scrapers, compressors, pumps, derricks, shovels, tractors, or front-end loaders to excavate, move, and grade earth, erect structures, or pour concrete or other hard surface pavement. May repair and maintain equipment in addition to other duties. Excludes “Extraction Workers” (47-5000) and “Crane and Tower Operators” (53-7021).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 47-2073.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Operating Engineers and Other Construction Equipment Operators is $59,850, which is 17% higher. Among the 60 occupations of the field Construction and Extraction, it ranks number 22 by median pay. With 478,090 jobs, it makes up about 31 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($66,290) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Operating Engineers and Other Construction Equipment Operators pay by state

The highest median pay was in Hawaii ($115,860), Illinois ($97,740) and New Jersey ($89,660). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($29,810), Guam ($39,170) and Arkansas ($44,520). The most jobs were in Texas (55,540), California (36,020) and Florida (27,510). The occupation is most concentrated in Wyoming, where its share of all jobs is 4.4 times the national share.
