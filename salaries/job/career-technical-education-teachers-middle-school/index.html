Career/Technical Education Teachers, Middle School: pay in the United States, May 2025

Career/Technical Education Teachers, Middle School in the United States earned a median wage of $65,030 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Teach occupational, vocational, career, or technical subjects to students at the middle, intermediate, or junior high school level. Excludes “Special Education Teachers” (25-2050) and “Substitute Teachers, Short-Term” (25-3031).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-2023.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Career/Technical Education Teachers, Middle School is $65,030, which is 28% higher. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 46 by median pay. With 16,870 jobs, it makes up about 1.1 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($71,590) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Career/Technical Education Teachers, Middle School pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($103,290), Rhode Island ($97,240) and Connecticut ($95,680). The lowest was in North Dakota ($46,530), North Carolina ($52,270) and Missouri ($52,630). The most jobs were in Texas (5,230), New York (1,930) and North Carolina (760). The occupation is most concentrated in North Dakota, where its share of all jobs is 4.8 times the national share.
