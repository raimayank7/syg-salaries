Engineering Teachers, Postsecondary: pay in the United States, May 2025

Engineering Teachers, Postsecondary in the United States earned a median wage of $109,270 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Teach courses pertaining to the application of physical laws and principles of engineering for the development of machines, materials, instruments, processes, and services. Includes teachers of subjects such as chemical, civil, electrical, industrial, mechanical, mineral, and petroleum engineering. Includes both teachers primarily engaged in teaching and those who do a combination of teaching and research. Excludes “Computer Science Teachers, Postsecondary” (25-1021).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-1032.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Engineering Teachers, Postsecondary is $109,270, which is 114% higher. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 3 by median pay. With 40,270 jobs, it makes up about 2.6 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($124,520) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Engineering Teachers, Postsecondary pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($137,280), Illinois ($135,430) and Massachusetts ($134,850). The lowest was in Pennsylvania ($82,070), Puerto Rico ($83,150) and Maine ($88,750). The most jobs were in Texas (4,080), New York (3,240) and Pennsylvania (2,990). The occupation is most concentrated in Massachusetts, where its share of all jobs is 2.8 times the national share.
