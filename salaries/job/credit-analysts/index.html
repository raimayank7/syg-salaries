Credit Analysts: pay in the United States, May 2025

Credit Analysts in the United States earned a median wage of $83,510 a year ($40.15 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Analyze credit data and financial statements of individuals or firms to determine the degree of risk involved in extending credit or lending money. Prepare reports with credit information for use in decisionmaking. Excludes “Financial Risk Specialists” (13-2054).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 13-2041.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Credit Analysts is $83,510, which is 64% higher. Among the 32 occupations of the field Business and Financial Operations, it ranks number 10 by median pay. With 64,390 jobs, it makes up about 4.1 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($100,850) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Credit Analysts pay by state

The highest median pay was in New York ($133,270), District of Columbia ($132,590) and Virginia ($102,430). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($51,920), West Virginia ($57,320) and Wyoming ($62,210). The most jobs were in New York (7,960), Texas (6,130) and California (5,260). The occupation is most concentrated in Delaware, where its share of all jobs is 3.6 times the national share.
