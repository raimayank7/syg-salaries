Taxi Drivers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Taxi Drivers in the United States earned a median wage of $42,100 a year ($20.24 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Drive a motor vehicle to transport passengers on an unplanned basis and charge a fare, usually based on a meter. Excludes “Shuttle Drivers and Chauffeurs” (53-3053).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 53-3054.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Taxi Drivers is $42,100, which is 17% lower. Among the 50 occupations of the field Transportation and Material Moving, it ranks number 36 by median pay. With 41,050 jobs, it makes up about 2.6 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($49,930) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Taxi Drivers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Texas ($48,740), Nevada ($44,800) and Colorado ($43,490). The lowest was in Kansas ($26,970), Wisconsin ($27,820) and Florida ($28,060). The most jobs were in California (3,880), Nevada (2,980) and Arizona (950). The occupation is most concentrated in Nevada, where its share of all jobs is 7.3 times the national share.
