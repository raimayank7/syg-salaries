Surveying and Mapping Technicians: pay in the United States, May 2025

Surveying and Mapping Technicians in the United States earned a median wage of $54,240 a year ($26.08 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Perform surveying and mapping duties, usually under the direction of an engineer, surveyor, cartographer, or photogrammetrist, to obtain data used for construction, mapmaking, boundary location, mining, or other purposes. May calculate mapmaking information and create maps from source data, such as surveying notes, aerial photography, satellite data, or other maps to show topographical features, political boundaries, and other features. May verify accuracy and completeness of maps. Excludes “Cartographers and Photogrammetrists” (17-1021), “Surveyors" (17-1022), and “Geoscientists, Except Hydrologists and Geographers” (19-2042).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 17-3031.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Surveying and Mapping Technicians is $54,240, which is 6% higher. Among the 36 occupations of the field Architecture and Engineering, it ranks number 36 by median pay. With 58,010 jobs, it makes up about 3.7 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Surveying and Mapping Technicians pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($78,110), Alaska ($76,970) and Minnesota ($73,190). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($32,370), South Carolina ($43,740) and Kentucky ($46,050). The most jobs were in Texas (8,720), Florida (4,810) and North Carolina (4,100). The occupation is most concentrated in Alaska, where its share of all jobs is 2.5 times the national share.
