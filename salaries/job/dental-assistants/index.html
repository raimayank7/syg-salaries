Dental Assistants: pay in the United States, May 2025

Dental Assistants in the United States earned a median wage of $48,070 a year ($23.11 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Perform limited clinical duties under the direction of a dentist. Clinical duties may include equipment preparation and sterilization, preparing patients for treatment, assisting the dentist during treatment, and providing patients with instructions for oral healthcare procedures. May perform administrative duties such as scheduling appointments, maintaining medical records, billing, and coding information for insurance purposes.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 31-9091.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Dental Assistants is $48,070, which is 6% lower. Among the 17 occupations of the field Healthcare Support, it ranks number 5 by median pay. With 387,790 jobs, it makes up about 25 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Dental Assistants pay by state

The highest median pay was in Minnesota ($65,270), District of Columbia ($60,580) and Maine ($60,070). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($35,910), Virgin Islands ($36,540) and Utah ($37,760). The most jobs were in California (56,220), Texas (33,230) and Florida (26,620). The occupation is most concentrated in Utah, where its share of all jobs is 1.5 times the national share.
