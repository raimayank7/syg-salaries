Teaching Assistants, Postsecondary: pay in the United States, May 2025

Teaching Assistants, Postsecondary in the United States earned a median wage of $42,910 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Assist faculty or other instructional staff in postsecondary institutions by performing instructional support activities, such as developing teaching materials, leading discussion groups, preparing and giving examinations, and grading examinations or papers. Graduate students who teach one or more full courses should be classified in the 25-1000 minor group.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-9044.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Teaching Assistants, Postsecondary is $42,910, which is 16% lower. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 61 by median pay. With 164,090 jobs, it makes up about 11 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($47,670) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Teaching Assistants, Postsecondary pay by state

The highest median pay was in Maryland ($58,330), Massachusetts ($56,370) and Texas ($55,670). The lowest was in Alabama ($22,600), Arkansas ($23,140) and Nevada ($24,960). The most jobs were in California (19,120), Michigan (18,440) and New York (17,390). The occupation is most concentrated in Michigan, where its share of all jobs is 4.0 times the national share.
