Radiation Therapists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Radiation Therapists in the United States earned a median wage of $105,310 a year ($50.63 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Provide radiation therapy to patients as prescribed by a radiation oncologist according to established practices and standards. Duties may include reviewing prescription and diagnosis; acting as liaison with physician and supportive care personnel; preparing equipment, such as immobilization, treatment, and protection devices; and maintaining records, reports, and files. May assist in dosimetry procedures and tumor localization.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-1124.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Radiation Therapists is $105,310, which is 107% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 33 by median pay. With 17,070 jobs, it makes up about 1.1 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Radiation Therapists pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($166,450), Oregon ($131,600) and New York ($129,620). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($37,560), West Virginia ($71,590) and Arkansas ($78,950). The most jobs were in California (1,720), Florida (1,280) and Texas (1,000). The occupation is most concentrated in Maine, where its share of all jobs is 2.0 times the national share.
