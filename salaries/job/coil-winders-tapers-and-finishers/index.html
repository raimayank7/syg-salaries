Coil Winders, Tapers, and Finishers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Coil Winders, Tapers, and Finishers in the United States earned a median wage of $48,220 a year ($23.18 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Wind wire coils used in electrical components, such as resistors and transformers, and in electrical equipment and instruments, such as field cores, bobbins, armature cores, electrical motors, generators, and control equipment.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 51-2021.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Coil Winders, Tapers, and Finishers is $48,220, which is 5% lower. Among the 105 occupations of the field Production, it ranks number 47 by median pay. With 12,840 jobs, it makes up about 0.8 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Coil Winders, Tapers, and Finishers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($73,920), Louisiana ($61,480) and Iowa ($59,780). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($21,840), Mississippi ($35,730) and Nevada ($38,140). The most jobs were in Tennessee (1,460), Texas (1,150) and Ohio (1,020). The occupation is most concentrated in Tennessee, where its share of all jobs is 5.4 times the national share.
