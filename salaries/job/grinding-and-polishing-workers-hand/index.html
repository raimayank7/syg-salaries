Grinding and Polishing Workers, Hand: pay in the United States, May 2025

Grinding and Polishing Workers, Hand in the United States earned a median wage of $42,660 a year ($20.51 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Grind, sand, or polish, using hand tools or hand-held power tools, a variety of metal, wood, stone, clay, plastic, or glass objects. Includes chippers, buffers, and finishers.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 51-9022.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Grinding and Polishing Workers, Hand is $42,660, which is 16% lower. Among the 105 occupations of the field Production, it ranks number 78 by median pay. With 10,510 jobs, it makes up about 0.7 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Grinding and Polishing Workers, Hand pay by state

The highest median pay was in Vermont ($56,850), Minnesota ($51,930) and Rhode Island ($51,630). The lowest was in New Mexico ($28,640), Louisiana ($33,160) and Tennessee ($33,830). The most jobs were in California (1,850), Texas (840) and Michigan (650). The occupation is most concentrated in Rhode Island, where its share of all jobs is 3.7 times the national share.
