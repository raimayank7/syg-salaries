Registered Nurses: pay in the United States, May 2025

Registered Nurses in the United States earned a median wage of $97,550 a year ($46.90 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Assess patient health problems and needs, develop and implement nursing care plans, and maintain medical records. Administer nursing care to ill, injured, convalescent, or disabled patients. May advise patients on health maintenance and disease prevention or provide case management. Licensing or registration required. Includes Clinical Nurse Specialists. Excludes “Nurse Anesthetists” (29-1151), “Nurse Midwives” (29-1161), and “Nurse Practitioners” (29-1171).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-1141.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Registered Nurses is $97,550, which is 91% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 40 by median pay. With 3,379,720 jobs, it makes up about 217 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Registered Nurses pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($140,270), Hawaii ($136,320) and Oregon ($129,010). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($39,880), Alabama ($77,080) and Mississippi ($77,090). The most jobs were in California (338,940), Texas (271,380) and Florida (229,940). The occupation is most concentrated in West Virginia, where its share of all jobs is 1.5 times the national share.
