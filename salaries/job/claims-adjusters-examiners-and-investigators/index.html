Claims Adjusters, Examiners, and Investigators: pay in the United States, May 2025

Claims Adjusters, Examiners, and Investigators in the United States earned a median wage of $78,000 a year ($37.50 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Review settled claims to determine that payments and settlements are made in accordance with company practices and procedures. Confer with legal counsel on claims requiring litigation. May also settle insurance claims. Excludes “Fire Inspectors and Investigators” (33-2021).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 13-1031.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Claims Adjusters, Examiners, and Investigators is $78,000, which is 53% higher. Among the 32 occupations of the field Business and Financial Operations, it ranks number 21 by median pay. With 324,230 jobs, it makes up about 21 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Claims Adjusters, Examiners, and Investigators pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($101,150), New Jersey ($95,690) and Maryland ($95,560). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($45,970), Guam ($50,560) and Virgin Islands ($60,050). The most jobs were in Texas (29,220), Florida (28,100) and California (24,290). The occupation is most concentrated in Arizona, where its share of all jobs is 2.0 times the national share.
