Licensed Practical and Licensed Vocational Nurses: pay in the United States, May 2025

Licensed Practical and Licensed Vocational Nurses in the United States earned a median wage of $64,400 a year ($30.96 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Care for ill, injured, or convalescing patients or persons with disabilities in hospitals, nursing homes, clinics, private homes, group homes, and similar institutions. May work under the supervision of a registered nurse. Licensing required.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-2061.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Licensed Practical and Licensed Vocational Nurses is $64,400, which is 26% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 57 by median pay. With 648,410 jobs, it makes up about 42 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Licensed Practical and Licensed Vocational Nurses pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($83,150), Alaska ($80,800) and Oregon ($80,470). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($30,760), Guam ($42,870) and Mississippi ($50,220). The most jobs were in California (82,850), Texas (57,560) and Ohio (39,900). The occupation is most concentrated in Louisiana, where its share of all jobs is 2.2 times the national share.
