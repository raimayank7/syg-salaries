Highway Maintenance Workers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Highway Maintenance Workers in the United States earned a median wage of $50,260 a year ($24.16 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Maintain highways, municipal and rural roads, airport runways, and rights-of-way. Duties include patching broken or eroded pavement and repairing guard rails, highway markers, and snow fences. May also mow or clear brush from along road, or plow snow from roadway. Excludes “Tree Trimmers and Pruners” (37-3013).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 47-4051.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Highway Maintenance Workers is $50,260, which is about the same. Among the 60 occupations of the field Construction and Extraction, it ranks number 42 by median pay. With 154,960 jobs, it makes up about 10.0 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Highway Maintenance Workers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Oregon ($65,330), Washington ($65,300) and Connecticut ($64,870). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($21,840), Mississippi ($35,120) and Arkansas ($35,740). The most jobs were in New York (13,820), Pennsylvania (11,140) and Illinois (10,790). The occupation is most concentrated in South Dakota, where its share of all jobs is 4.0 times the national share.
