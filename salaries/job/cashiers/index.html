Cashiers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Cashiers in the United States earned a median wage of $32,880 a year ($15.81 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Receive and disburse money in establishments other than financial institutions. May use electronic scanners, cash registers, or related equipment. May process credit or debit card transactions and validate checks. Excludes “Gambling Change Persons and Booth Cashiers” (41-2012).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 41-2011.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Cashiers is $32,880, which is 36% lower. Among the 22 occupations of the field Sales and Related, it ranks number 22 by median pay. With 3,089,410 jobs, it makes up about 199 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Cashiers pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($38,880), Washington ($38,720) and California ($37,100). The lowest was in Guam ($21,610), Puerto Rico ($22,140) and West Virginia ($26,370). The most jobs were in California (336,110), Texas (245,730) and Florida (201,370). The occupation is most concentrated in Puerto Rico, where its share of all jobs is 1.5 times the national share.
