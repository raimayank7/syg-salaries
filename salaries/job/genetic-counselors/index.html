Genetic Counselors: pay in the United States, May 2025

Genetic Counselors in the United States earned a median wage of $100,040 a year ($48.09 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Assess individual or family risk for a variety of inherited conditions, such as genetic disorders and birth defects. Provide information to other healthcare providers or to individuals and families concerned with the risk of inherited conditions. Advise individuals and families to support informed decisionmaking and coping methods for those at risk. May help conduct research related to genetic conditions or genetic counseling.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-9092.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Genetic Counselors is $100,040, which is 96% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 37 by median pay. With 3,740 jobs, it makes up about 0.2 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Genetic Counselors pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($137,650), New Jersey ($117,750) and New York ($107,450). The lowest was in Georgia ($82,750), Missouri ($83,980) and Michigan ($84,970). The most jobs were in California (440), Tennessee (340) and New Jersey (240). The occupation is most concentrated in Tennessee, where its share of all jobs is 4.4 times the national share.
