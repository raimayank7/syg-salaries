Helpers--Installation, Maintenance, and Repair Workers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Helpers--Installation, Maintenance, and Repair Workers in the United States earned a median wage of $39,630 a year ($19.05 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Help installation, maintenance, and repair workers in maintenance, parts replacement, and repair of vehicles, industrial machinery, and electrical and electronic equipment. Perform duties such as furnishing tools, materials, and supplies to other workers; cleaning work area, machines, and tools; and holding materials or tools for other workers.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 49-9098.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Helpers--Installation, Maintenance, and Repair Workers is $39,630, which is 22% lower. Among the 51 occupations of the field Installation, Maintenance, and Repair, it ranks number 50 by median pay. With 95,580 jobs, it makes up about 6.1 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Helpers--Installation, Maintenance, and Repair Workers pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($54,860), Hawaii ($46,320) and Washington ($46,080). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($23,810), Guam ($27,050) and Wyoming ($31,260). The most jobs were in Texas (16,610), California (9,050) and Florida (5,520). The occupation is most concentrated in Guam, where its share of all jobs is 8.3 times the national share.
