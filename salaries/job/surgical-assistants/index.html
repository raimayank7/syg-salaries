Surgical Assistants: pay in the United States, May 2025

Surgical Assistants in the United States earned a median wage of $66,800 a year ($32.11 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Assist in operations, under the supervision of surgeons. May, in accordance with state laws, help surgeons to make incisions and close surgical sites, manipulate or remove tissues, implant surgical devices or drains, suction the surgical site, place catheters, clamp or cauterize vessels or tissue, and apply dressings to surgical site. Excludes “Registered Nurses” (29-1141) and “Surgical Technologists” (29-2055).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-9093.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Surgical Assistants is $66,800, which is 31% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 53 by median pay. With 22,270 jobs, it makes up about 1.4 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Surgical Assistants pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($123,390), Arizona ($111,170) and Colorado ($107,440). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($30,160), Oklahoma ($40,160) and Alabama ($41,710). The most jobs were in Texas (2,840), Illinois (2,300) and Florida (1,260). The occupation is most concentrated in West Virginia, where its share of all jobs is 2.7 times the national share.
