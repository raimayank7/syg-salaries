Emergency Management Directors: pay in the United States, May 2025

Emergency Management Directors in the United States earned a median wage of $93,330 a year ($44.87 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Plan and direct disaster response or crisis management activities, provide disaster preparedness training, and prepare emergency plans and procedures for natural (e.g., hurricanes, floods, earthquakes), wartime, or technological (e.g., nuclear power plant emergencies or hazardous materials spills) disasters or hostage situations.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 11-9161.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Emergency Management Directors is $93,330, which is 83% higher. Among the 37 occupations of the field Management, it ranks number 27 by median pay. With 13,500 jobs, it makes up about 0.9 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Emergency Management Directors pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($184,350), New Mexico ($145,550) and Washington ($130,780). The lowest was in Mississippi ($48,650), Arkansas ($50,180) and West Virginia ($60,460). The most jobs were in Texas (1,210), California (1,170) and New York (790). The occupation is most concentrated in Alaska, where its share of all jobs is 4.4 times the national share.

Common questions

How much do Emergency Management Directors earn in the United States?

The median pay was $93,330 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. The lowest-paid 10% earned less than $54,210 and the highest-paid 10% more than $166,430.

How many people work as Emergency Management Directors?

About 13,500 jobs in the United States in May 2025, not counting the self-employed.
