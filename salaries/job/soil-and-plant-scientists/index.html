Soil and Plant Scientists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Soil and Plant Scientists in the United States earned a median wage of $78,850 a year ($37.91 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Conduct research in breeding, physiology, production, yield, and management of crops and agricultural plants or trees, shrubs, and nursery stock, their growth in soils, and control of pests; or study the chemical, physical, biological, and mineralogical composition of soils as they relate to plant or crop growth. May classify and map soils and investigate effects of alternative practices on soil and crop productivity.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 19-1013.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Soil and Plant Scientists is $78,850, which is 55% higher. Among the 48 occupations of the field Life, Physical, and Social Science, it ranks number 29 by median pay. With 15,730 jobs, it makes up about 1.0 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($87,720) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Soil and Plant Scientists pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($107,140), Florida ($103,240) and Alaska ($100,320). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($38,980), Wyoming ($55,680) and Ohio ($56,820). The most jobs were in California (1,440), Iowa (960) and Illinois (820). The occupation is most concentrated in South Dakota, where its share of all jobs is 10.3 times the national share.
