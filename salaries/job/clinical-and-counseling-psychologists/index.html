Clinical and Counseling Psychologists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Clinical and Counseling Psychologists in the United States earned a median wage of $100,580 a year ($48.36 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Assess, diagnose, and treat mental and emotional disorders of individuals through observation, interview, and psychological tests. Help individuals with distress or maladjustment understand their problems through their knowledge of case history, interviews with patients, and theory. Provide individual or group counseling services to assist individuals in achieving more effective personal, social, educational, and vocational development and adjustment. May design behavior modification programs and consult with medical personnel regarding the best treatment for patients. Excludes “Psychiatrists” (29-1223).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 19-3033.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Clinical and Counseling Psychologists is $100,580, which is 97% higher. Among the 48 occupations of the field Life, Physical, and Social Science, it ranks number 16 by median pay. With 75,990 jobs, it makes up about 4.9 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($112,750) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Clinical and Counseling Psychologists pay by state

The highest median pay was in Oregon ($134,350), New Jersey ($127,090) and New Mexico ($124,800). The lowest was in New Hampshire ($64,270), Puerto Rico ($67,660) and Montana ($68,160). The most jobs were in California (12,840), New York (8,420) and Illinois (5,810). The occupation is most concentrated in West Virginia, where its share of all jobs is 2.7 times the national share.
