Library Technicians: pay in the United States, May 2025

Library Technicians in the United States earned a median wage of $44,580 a year ($21.43 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Assist librarians by helping readers in the use of library catalogs, databases, and indexes to locate books and other materials; and by answering questions that require only brief consultation of standard reference. Compile records; sort and shelve books or other media; remove or repair damaged books or other media; register patrons; and check materials in and out of the circulation process. Replace materials in shelving area (stacks) or files. Includes bookmobile drivers who assist with providing services in mobile libraries.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-4031.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Library Technicians is $44,580, which is 13% lower. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 59 by median pay. With 68,690 jobs, it makes up about 4.4 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Library Technicians pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($62,570), District of Columbia ($61,040) and Connecticut ($57,060). The lowest was in Wyoming ($26,850), Mississippi ($27,080) and Arkansas ($28,570). The most jobs were in California (7,190), Texas (5,180) and Ohio (4,020). The occupation is most concentrated in South Dakota, where its share of all jobs is 3.2 times the national share.
