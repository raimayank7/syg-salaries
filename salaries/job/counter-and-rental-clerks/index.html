Counter and Rental Clerks: pay in the United States, May 2025

Counter and Rental Clerks in the United States earned a median wage of $41,300 a year ($19.86 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Receive orders, generally in person, for repairs, rentals, and services. May describe available options, compute cost, and accept payment. Excludes “Fast Food and Counter Workers” (35-3023), “Hotel, Motel, and Resort Desk Clerks” (43-4081), “Order Clerks” (43-4151), and “Reservation and Transportation Ticket Agents and Travel Clerks” (43-4181).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 41-2021.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Counter and Rental Clerks is $41,300, which is 19% lower. Among the 22 occupations of the field Sales and Related, it ranks number 16 by median pay. With 400,810 jobs, it makes up about 26 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($45,700) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Counter and Rental Clerks pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($53,760), New Jersey ($48,900) and Vermont ($48,430). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($23,110), Guam ($23,600) and Virgin Islands ($29,560). The most jobs were in California (67,770), Texas (36,600) and Florida (27,160). The occupation is most concentrated in Colorado, where its share of all jobs is 1.9 times the national share.
