Cleaners of Vehicles and Equipment: pay in the United States, May 2025

Cleaners of Vehicles and Equipment in the United States earned a median wage of $35,830 a year ($17.23 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Wash or otherwise clean vehicles, machinery, and other equipment. Use such materials as water, cleaning agents, brushes, cloths, and hoses. Excludes “Janitors and Cleaners, Except Maids and Housekeeping Cleaners” (37-2011).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 53-7061.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Cleaners of Vehicles and Equipment is $35,830, which is 30% lower. Among the 50 occupations of the field Transportation and Material Moving, it ranks number 47 by median pay. With 380,430 jobs, it makes up about 24 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Cleaners of Vehicles and Equipment pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($42,710), Alaska ($40,210) and Washington ($40,070). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($21,890), Guam ($21,980) and Louisiana ($27,600). The most jobs were in California (46,030), Texas (37,750) and Florida (24,920). The occupation is most concentrated in Kansas, where its share of all jobs is 1.8 times the national share.
