Photographers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Photographers in the United States earned a median wage of $44,660 a year ($21.47 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Photograph people, landscapes, merchandise, or other subjects. May use lighting equipment to enhance a subject’s appearance. May use editing software to produce finished images and prints. Includes commercial and industrial photographers, scientific photographers, and photojournalists. Excludes “Camera Operators, Television, Video, and Film” (27-4031).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 27-4021.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Photographers is $44,660, which is 12% lower. Among the 41 occupations of the field Arts, Design, Entertainment, Sports, and Media, it ranks number 37 by median pay. With 51,760 jobs, it makes up about 3.3 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($55,410) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Photographers pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($110,970), New York ($60,050) and Massachusetts ($57,990). The lowest was in Arkansas ($32,810), Idaho ($33,320) and Puerto Rico ($34,060). The most jobs were in California (7,980), Florida (4,390) and Texas (4,330). The occupation is most concentrated in Hawaii, where its share of all jobs is 2.1 times the national share.
