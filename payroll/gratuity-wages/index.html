Wages for gratuity: the 50% rule

Gratuity is worked on "wages" as defined in section 2(88) of the Code on Social Security, 2020 (read on 13 November 2026). Wages mean basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance. The listed other payments are not wages, but if they add up to more than one-half of all remuneration, the excess is added to wages. PIB describes the effect as raising the wage on which gratuity is worked.

Example

Basic pay ₹20,000 and other payments ₹40,000 make ₹60,000. Half is ₹30,000, so the wages are ₹20,000 plus (₹40,000 − ₹30,000) = ₹30,000. With basic ₹40,000 and other payments ₹20,000 the other payments are under half, so wages stay ₹40,000.

What the section leaves out of wages

  • bonus under any law that is not part of the remuneration in the terms of employment;
  • the value of house accommodation, light, water, medical attendance and similar amenities;
  • the employer’s contribution to a pension or provident fund and interest on it;
  • conveyance allowance or travelling concession;
  • sums paid for special expenses of the job;
  • house rent allowance;
  • remuneration under an award, settlement or court order;
  • overtime allowance and commission;
  • gratuity, retrenchment compensation and other payments at the end of employment.

Common questions

What counts as wages for gratuity?

Section 2(88) of the Code on Social Security: basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance. House rent allowance, conveyance, overtime, commission and the other listed payments are left out, except for the part that exceeds one-half of all remuneration, which is added back.
