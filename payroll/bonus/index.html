Bonus: section 26 of the Code on Wages

Chapter IV of the Code on Wages, 2019 deals with bonus (text read on 13 November 2026). The minimum is eight and one-third per cent of the wages earned in the accounting year or ₹100, whichever is higher, for an employee who has worked at least thirty days. Wages above the limit are worked as if they were the limit.

The wage limit is a notification, not in the Code

Section 26(1) applies to employees "drawing wages not exceeding such amount per mensem, as determined by notification" of the appropriate Government, and section 26(2) says that where wages exceed that amount, the bonus is calculated as if the wage were that amount or the minimum wage fixed by the appropriate Government, whichever is higher. The Code gives no figure, so the calculator asks you for it.

Example

Wages of ₹40,000 a month with a limit of ₹21,000 and a minimum wage of ₹10,000: the wage is worked at ₹21,000, so for 12 months at 8.33% the bonus is ₹20,992.

Not worked here

The proportionate reduction for absence (section 27), the computation of working days (section 28), disqualification (section 29) and the allocable surplus (section 36) are not worked here.

Common questions

What is the minimum bonus?

Section 26(1) of the Code on Wages: eight and one-third per cent of the wages earned, or ₹100, whichever is higher, for an employee who has worked at least thirty days in the accounting year, whether or not the employer has an allocable surplus.

What is the maximum?

Where the allocable surplus is greater than the minimum bonus the employer pays a proportionate bonus of up to twenty per cent of the wages (section 26(3)); a bonus agreed on production or productivity can raise the total, but not above twenty per cent (section 26(5)).
