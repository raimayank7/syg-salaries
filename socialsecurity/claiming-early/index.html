Social Security Claiming Age

Starting benefits early

You can start Social Security retirement benefits as early as 62, but you are entitled to the full amount only at your full retirement age. The SSA page read on 13 November 2026 gives this table:

If you were born on the 1st of a month, SSA figures your benefit and full retirement age as if your birthday were in the previous month; born on 1 January means the previous December. The percentages are approximate because of rounding.

Spouse’s benefit

The most a spouse can get is 50% of the benefit the worker would receive at full retirement age; the early-claiming cut is applied after that 50%. SSA’s table shows $500 becoming $350 for those born 1943 to 1954 (a 30% cut) and $325 for those born 1960 or later (35%).

Common questions

Do I get a smaller benefit for life if I start early?

The SSA page says your benefits will be reduced a small percentage for each month before your full retirement age.

How early can I start?

As early as age 62, for the whole month: you must be at least 62 for the entire month.
