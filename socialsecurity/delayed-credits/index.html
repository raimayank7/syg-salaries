Waiting past full retirement age

The SSA page read on 13 November 2026 says retirement benefits are increased for each month you delay starting them beyond full retirement age, and the increase stops at 70. For people born in 1943 or later the rate is 8% for each 12 months, which is two-thirds of 1% a month. (Earlier birth years have lower rates: 5.5% for 1933-34 up to 7.5% for 1941-42.)

Common questions

Does the increase continue after 70?

No. The benefit increase stops when you reach age 70, so there is no gain from waiting longer.

Do I still need Medicare at 65?

SSA advises signing up for Medicare at 65 even if you delay retirement benefits, because coverage may be delayed and cost more otherwise.
