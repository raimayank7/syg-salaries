Land and Buildings Transaction Tax in Scotland

Scotland charges Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT), collected by Revenue Scotland, instead of Stamp Duty Land Tax. The residential rates below are from the Revenue Scotland page read on 13 November 2026; the page was revised on 28 August 2026 and shows the rates as of 1 April 2021.

First-time buyers

The first band is raised from £145,000 to £175,000, so the tax is nothing up to £175,000 and the other bands are unchanged. At £250,000 that is £1,500 for a first-time buyer instead of £2,100.

Additional Dwelling Supplement

When you buy an additional residential property for £40,000 or more, an extra 8% of the whole price is added. At £250,000 the LBTT is £2,100 and the supplement is £20,000, so £22,100 in all. The 8% applies to transactions on or after 5 December 2024 unless the contract was made on or before 4 December 2024, when the earlier 6% applies. You can claim a repayment if you sell your previous main home within 36 months (18 months before 1 April 2024), and live in the new one as your only or main residence.

Common questions

What is the Additional Dwelling Supplement?

An extra 8% of the whole price, added to any LBTT, when you buy an additional residential property in Scotland (transactions from 5 December 2024; Revenue Scotland says it was 6% before). It applies when the price is £40,000 or more.

Is there first-time buyer relief in Scotland?

Yes: Revenue Scotland says the nil-rate band rises from £145,000 to £175,000 for a first-time buyer, so the relief is worth at most £600.
