Which Income-tax Act applies?

The Income-tax Act, 2025 came into force on 1 April 2026 (section 1(3)) and repealed the Income-tax Act, 1961 (section 536(1)). The two Acts sit side by side for a time, because the 1961 Act still governs earlier years. This page sets out what the Act and the CBDT's transition FAQs say.

What it means for section numbers

  • If you are filing, paying or answering a notice for FY 2025-26 or earlier, the old section numbers (80C, 139, 234A and so on) still apply.
  • For tax year 2026-27, quote the new section (123 for the 80C deduction, 263 for returns, 423 for interest on a late return).
  • TDS rates and thresholds were not changed; section 393 presents the old TDS sections in one table (FAQ Q6.3).

Common questions

Does the 1961 Act stop applying on 1 April 2026?

The Income-tax Act, 1961 is repealed (section 536(1)) but, irrespective of the repeal, nothing affects its previous operation and rights and liabilities under it, and its provisions continue to apply to pending proceedings and to proceedings begun on or after 1 April 2026 for any tax year beginning before 1 April 2026 (section 536(2)(a) to (c), read from the Act).

What law covers income of FY 2025-26?

The 1961 Act: income earned from 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026 is assessed for assessment year 2026-27 under it, and income from 1 April 2026 under the 2025 Act as tax year 2026-27 (CBDT FAQs, Q1.12).

Which Act governs TDS in the switch?

The date of the earlier of credit or payment decides: up to 31 March 2026 the 1961 Act, from 1 April 2026 the 2025 Act (Q6.1); rates and thresholds were retained (Q6.3).

How many sections does the new Act have?

536 sections and 16 schedules, against 819 sections and 14 schedules in the 1961 Act (FAQ Q1.5).
