DA arrears: how they work and how much

When the Cabinet approves a DA rise months after its date, the difference for the months in between is paid as arrears. This page shows how big the arrears are for a basic pay of ₹56,100 at each rise since January 2024.

The rates filled in are the latest two in the Cabinet decisions: 63% from 1 July 2026, replacing 60%. Change them to try any other rate or period. Arrears = months × the monthly increase.

How to work out your own

  1. Take your basic pay for each month of the period.
  2. Work out DA at the new rate and at the old rate for each month.
  3. Add up the differences.

If your basic pay changed during the period (for example an annual increment), use the basic pay of each month, not one figure. The calculator multiplies one basic pay by the number of months; for a changed basic pay run it once for each part of the period.

The 3-point rise from 1 July 2026 was approved on 28 October 2026, so on a basic pay of ₹56,100 DA is ₹1,683 a month more, which is ₹6,732 for July to October. The release does not say when the arrears are paid.

Common questions

How are DA arrears worked out?

Months from the date the rate applies to the date it is first paid, multiplied by the monthly increase (new DA minus old DA on the same basic pay).

Are arrears taxed?

Arrears are part of income; tax treatment depends on your return. This site does not work that out.
