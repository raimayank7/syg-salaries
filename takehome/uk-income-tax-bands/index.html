UK Income Tax bands, 2026-27

The tax year runs from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. The standard Personal Allowance is £12,570. Bands below apply to taxable income, which is income above the allowance.

Personal Allowance above £100,000

The allowance goes down by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £100,000 and is nil at £125,140 or more. Read what that does to your pay.

GOV.UK also shows the bands as income bands for people with a full allowance: 20% from £12,571 to £50,270, 40% from £50,271 to £125,140 and 45% above; this is the same thing described on income instead of taxable income.

A worked example: a salary of £60,000 in England

The Personal Allowance is £12,570, so the taxable income is £47,430. The first £37,700 is taxed at 20%, which is £7,540. The rest, £9,730, is taxed at 40%, which is £3,892. The Income Tax for the year is £11,432. National Insurance is separate: see National Insurance.

The same salary in Scotland

With the Scottish bands the Income Tax on £60,000 is £13,182, which is £1,750 more than in England. Scottish bands apply to people who live in Scotland as defined by HMRC; this page does not decide who that is.
