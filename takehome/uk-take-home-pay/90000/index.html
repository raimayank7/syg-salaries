Take-home pay on a £90,000 salary

On a salary of £90,000 a year in the 2026-27 tax year, you keep £62,757 a year, which is £5,229.78 a month or £1,206.87 a week, if you live in England, Wales or Northern Ireland, have no pension and no student loan. That is 69.7% of your salary.

In Scotland

Scotland has its own Income Tax bands. On the same salary the tax is £26,232 and the take-home pay is £59,957 a year, £4,996.45 a month, which is £233.34 less a month than in the rest of the UK.

What an extra £1,000 does

If your salary rose by £1,000, you would keep £580 of it (£420 tax and National Insurance on that £1,000).

Assumptions

A steady yearly salary from one job in the 2026-27 tax year, standard tax code, National Insurance category A, no pension contribution, no benefits in kind and no other income. Student loan is taken as 9% of earnings above the yearly threshold of the plan (HMRC works it per pay period). Scottish bands are used only where the page says Scotland. Take-home pay on your payslip can differ.

Common questions

How much is a £90,000 salary after tax?

In 2026-27, with no pension or student loan: £62,757 a year, £5,229.78 a month in England, Wales and Northern Ireland; £4,996.45 a month in Scotland.

What is the tax on £90,000?

Income Tax £23,432 and National Insurance £3,811, a total of £27,243 (England).
