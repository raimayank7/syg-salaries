Take-home pay on a £20,000 salary

On a salary of £20,000 a year in the 2026-27 tax year, you keep £17,920 a year, which is £1,493.30 a month or £344.61 a week, if you live in England, Wales or Northern Ireland, have no pension and no student loan. That is 89.6% of your salary.

In Scotland

Scotland has its own Income Tax bands. On the same salary the tax is £1,446 and the take-home pay is £17,959 a year, £1,496.61 a month, which is £3.31 more a month than in the rest of the UK.

What an extra £1,000 does

If your salary rose by £1,000, you would keep £720 of it (£280 tax and National Insurance on that £1,000).

Assumptions

A steady yearly salary from one job in the 2026-27 tax year, standard tax code, National Insurance category A, no pension contribution, no benefits in kind and no other income. Student loan is taken as 9% of earnings above the yearly threshold of the plan (HMRC works it per pay period). Scottish bands are used only where the page says Scotland. Take-home pay on your payslip can differ.

Common questions

How much is a £20,000 salary after tax?

In 2026-27, with no pension or student loan: £17,920 a year, £1,493.30 a month in England, Wales and Northern Ireland; £1,496.61 a month in Scotland.

What is the tax on £20,000?

Income Tax £1,486 and National Insurance £594, a total of £2,080 (England).
