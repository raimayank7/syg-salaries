Reading the table

  • The first columns are for England, Wales and Northern Ireland; the last column uses the Scottish bands.
  • Between £50,270 and £100,000 a pound of extra salary keeps 58p: 40% tax plus 2% National Insurance.
  • Between £100,000 and £125,140 the loss of the Personal Allowance makes the rate on extra pay 62% (see the £100,000 trap).

Assumptions

A steady yearly salary from one job in the 2026-27 tax year, standard tax code, National Insurance category A, no pension contribution, no benefits in kind and no other income. Student loan is taken as 9% of earnings above the yearly threshold of the plan (HMRC works it per pay period). Scottish bands are used only where the page says Scotland. Take-home pay on your payslip can differ.

Sources

  • Income Tax rates, Personal Allowance and its reduction above £100,000, for the tax year 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027: GOV.UK, "Income Tax rates and Personal Allowances" (gov.uk/income-tax-rates), read on 13 November 2026.
  • Tax bands for England, Wales, Northern Ireland and Scotland, Class 1 employee National Insurance (category A) and student loan thresholds, 2026 to 2027: GOV.UK, "Rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027" (gov.uk), read on 13 November 2026.

Rates read on 13 November 2026 from GOV.UK and apply to the tax year 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027; after 5 April 2027 they will be out of date. This site is independent, not connected with HMRC, and gives arithmetic, not tax advice.
