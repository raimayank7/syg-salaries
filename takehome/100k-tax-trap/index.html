The £100,000 tax trap

GOV.UK says the Personal Allowance goes down by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £100,000, and is nil at £125,140 or more. So between those two points each extra £2 of income loses £1 of allowance, which is taxed at 40%.

The arithmetic

For each extra £1 between £100,000 and £125,140: 40p tax on the £1 itself, plus 20p tax because 50p of allowance disappears (50p × 40%), plus 2p National Insurance. That is 62p of every extra £1 and you keep 38p. A person on £110,000 has a Personal Allowance of £7,570 and keeps £72,357 of the £110,000.

What people do

The tax is on adjusted net income, which is income after some deductions such as pension contributions through certain schemes. Paying into a pension may reduce adjusted net income, which can restore some allowance; the rules are on GOV.UK and a pension has its own conditions, so this site does not model it.
