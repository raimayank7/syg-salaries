Employee National Insurance, 2026-27

Employees pay Class 1 National Insurance on earnings. For the most common category (A) in 2026-27 the rates from GOV.UK are:

Yearly earningsRate
Up to the primary threshold, £12,5700%
£12,570 to the upper earnings limit, £50,2708%
Above £50,2702%

Notes

  • HMRC works NI per pay period, so it can differ a little from a yearly calculation if pay changes during the year.
  • Other categories (for example for people under 21 or apprentices, with their own thresholds for the employer) are listed on GOV.UK; this site uses category A.
  • Employer National Insurance is paid by the employer on top of your salary and is not deducted from your pay.

A worked example

On a salary of £40,000: the first £12,570 has no National Insurance; the next £27,430 is charged at 8%, which is £2,194. On £60,000 the band from £12,570 to £50,270 gives £3,016 at 8% and the £9,730 above gives £195 at 2%, a total of £3,211. Notice that National Insurance rises less than Income Tax once earnings pass £50,270, because the rate drops from 8% to 2%.

Who this covers

The figures are for employees of category A. Self-employed people pay different classes and the employer rates are not shown here. Check GOV.UK for your own category letter on your payslip.
