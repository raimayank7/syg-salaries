UPI Limits and Rules

RBI rules for failed digital payments

RBI circular DPSS.CO.PD No.629/02.01.014/2019-20 of 20 September 2019 sets one framework for what banks must do when a payment fails and for how much they owe you, effective 15 October 2019. These are its terms, read on 13 November 2026.

The terms

  • T is the calendar day of the transaction, and R is the day the reversal is concluded and the money reaches the originating bank, which must credit you the same day.
  • The time limit is the outer limit: banks and operators should resolve faster where they can.
  • Compensation is credited to your account on its own, without waiting for a complaint or claim.
  • If you do not get redress within the time limit you can complain under the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021.
  • Prepaid wallets and cards follow the system the payment rides on (UPI, card network, IMPS) when it goes to another issuer.

An example

A UPI transfer is debited on 1 June but the other person is not credited and the money returns on 6 June. The limit is T + 1, so five days are late: ₹500. If a merchant payment is debited on 1 June, no confirmation reaches the merchant and the money returns on 12 June, the limit is T + 5, so six days are late: ₹600.

NPCI’s dispute rules

NPCI circular OC 240 of 4 November 2026 changes dispute-handling terms between banks: "Wrong Credit Chargeback" becomes "Wrong Payment - Reversal Request", and that request and "Fraud Chargeback" are now allowed for deemed-approved transactions after the transaction closing updates. These are rules between banks and are named so you recognise them if your bank uses them.

Common questions

What counts as a failed transaction?

RBI defines it as a transaction not fully completed for a reason not attributable to the customer, such as a failure in communication links, no cash in an ATM or a session time-out. It also includes credits that could not be made for lack of full or proper information and delay in starting a reversal.

Does this cover payments to people abroad?

No. RBI says the framework covers domestic transactions, where both the sender and the beneficiary are within India.
