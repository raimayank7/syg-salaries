Residential status of an individual: section 6

Section 6 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 decides whether an individual is resident in India for a tax year. These are its rules in plain words, as read on 13 November 2026. The checker on the home page applies them in this order.

1. The basic tests, section 6(2)

  • 182 days: you are resident if you are in India for 182 days or more in the tax year.
  • 60 and 365 days: you are also resident if you are in India for 60 days or more in the year and have been in India for 365 days or more in the four tax years before it.

2. Two exceptions to the 60-day limb, sections 6(3) to 6(5)

  • A citizen who leaves India in the tax year as crew of an Indian ship or for employment outside India: the 60-day and 365-day test does not apply, so only 182 days counts.
  • A citizen or person of Indian origin who lives outside India and comes on a visit: the 60-day and 365-day test does not apply, unless the person’s total income other than income from foreign sources exceeds ₹15 lakh, in which case it applies with 120 days instead of 60.

3. Deemed resident, section 6(7)

A citizen is deemed resident if not liable to tax in any other country because of domicile, residence or a similar test, and total income other than from foreign sources exceeds ₹15 lakh. This does not apply to someone already resident under the tests above. See the 15 lakh rule.

4. Then: ordinarily resident or not, section 6(13)

A resident is "not ordinarily resident" if any of these is true: non-resident in nine of the ten preceding tax years; 729 days or fewer in India in the seven preceding tax years; a citizen or person of Indian origin with income above ₹15 lakh who was in India for 120 days or more but fewer than 182; or a deemed resident under 6(7). Everyone else who is resident is "resident and ordinarily resident". See RNOR.

Who is a person of Indian origin

Section 2(78): an individual who, or either of whose parents or any of whose grandparents, was born in undivided India.

Common questions

Does leaving for a job abroad change the 60-day rule?

Yes. Section 6(3) says the 60-day limb in 6(2)(b) does not apply to a citizen who leaves India in the tax year for employment outside India or as a member of the crew of an Indian ship, so only the 182-day test can make such a person resident that year.
