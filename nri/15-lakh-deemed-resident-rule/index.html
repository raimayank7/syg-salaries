NRI Tax Status

The ₹15 lakh rules for NRIs

The figure ₹15 lakh appears in three places in section 6 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. In each, the amount is the person’s total income other than income from foreign sources for the tax year (read on 13 November 2026).

Visiting citizens and persons of Indian origin, 6(4) and 6(5)

A citizen or person of Indian origin who lives outside India and comes to India on a visit is not caught by the 60-day and 365-day test. If the income exceeds ₹15 lakh, the test applies, but with 120 days instead of 60. Example: 130 days in India and 400 days in the four earlier years makes a visitor with ₹20 lakh of Indian income a resident, and one with ₹10 lakh a non-resident (Resident but not ordinarily resident (RNOR) and Non-resident (NR) in the checker).

The deemed resident citizen, 6(7)

A citizen who is not liable to tax in any other country, because of domicile, residence or a similar criterion, and whose total income other than foreign-source income exceeds ₹15 lakh, is deemed resident in India even with few days in the country. Such a person is always "not ordinarily resident", section 6(13)(c), so is taxed on Indian income and on foreign income only from a business controlled in or a profession set up in India. Example: a citizen with ₹20 lakh of Indian income who spent 30 days in India and pays tax nowhere else: Resident but not ordinarily resident (RNOR).

The RNOR case, 6(13)(b)

A citizen or person of Indian origin with income above ₹15 lakh who was in India for 120 days or more but fewer than 182 days is resident but not ordinarily resident.

Common questions

Does the 15 lakh rule count foreign income?

No. Both in section 6(5) and in section 6(7) the ₹15 lakh is total income other than income from foreign sources. Section 6(14) says income from foreign sources is income accruing or arising outside India, except income from a business controlled in or a profession set up in India, and not deemed to accrue in India.
