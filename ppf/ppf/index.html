The PPF rules in short

  • Term: 15 years (the account can be extended in blocks of five years).
  • Limits: Minimum ₹500 and maximum ₹1,50,000 in a financial year.
  • Tax: Interest is tax free; deposits qualify for the income tax rebate under section 80C (as the NSI page words it).
  • Withdrawal and loan are available after the prescribed period, and the account can be closed early under conditions.

Why the 5th of the month matters

PPF interest for a month is worked out on the lowest balance in the account between the 5th and the end of that month. A deposit made after the 5th does not earn interest for that month. That is why people who deposit once a year try to pay in by 5 April, the first month of the financial year: the whole deposit then earns interest for all twelve months.

Common questions

What is the PPF interest rate now?

The official table of the National Savings Institute shows 7.1% a year for every quarter from April 2022 to July-September 2026. The rate is reviewed every quarter.

How much can I put into PPF in a year?

At least ₹500 and at most ₹1,50,000 in a financial year, as per the NSI scheme summary.

Is PPF interest taxed?

The NSI summary calls the interest tax free and says deposits qualify for the section 80C rebate. The new tax regime does not allow that deduction; see Tax Slabs.

For how long can I keep a PPF account?

The term is 15 years and, as the NSI summary says, the account can be extended in blocks of five years.
