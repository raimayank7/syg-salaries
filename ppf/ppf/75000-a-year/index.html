PPF: ₹75,000 a year for 15 years

This page shows what a PPF account holds if you put in ₹75,000 every year, in one payment by 5 April, for the full 15 years at the official rate of 7.1%. You put in ₹11,25,000 in all. The balance at the end of year 15 is ₹20,34,106, of which ₹9,09,106 is interest.

What this tells you

  • The interest in the last year alone is ₹1,34,847, more than the whole first-year interest of ₹5,325, because interest is earned on a growing balance.
  • Of the final balance, 55% is your own money and 45% is interest.
  • This is within the yearly limit of ₹1,50,000.
  • The rate of 7.1% is the latest in the official table. It is reviewed every quarter, so the real result can differ; the calculator lets you try another rate.

Depositing monthly instead

If you pay ₹6,250 every month by the 5th instead, you put in the same ₹75,000 a year but the balance after 15 years is ₹19,72,298, because the money goes in later in the year and earns less interest. Paying the whole amount by 5 April is the better way when you can.

Before you invest

Before you open or fund a PPF account, check three things at the post office or bank: the rate in force for the current quarter, the date by which a monthly deposit must be made to count for that month, and what happens if you miss the minimum deposit in a year. The scheme is long-term by design, so decide how much of your savings you can leave untouched for 15 years before you commit.

Common questions

How much will ₹75,000 a year in PPF become?

About ₹20,34,106 after 15 years at 7.1%, if the whole amount is paid by 5 April each year.

Is that guaranteed?

The rate is set by the government for each quarter and can change, so the final figure can be higher or lower.

  • PPF interest rule: Public Provident Fund Scheme, 2019, paragraph 7, as published by the National Savings Institute (nsiindia.gov.in), read on 13 November 2026: the lowest balance between the close of the fifth day and the end of the month earns interest, at a rate per annum, and interest is credited at the end of each year. The same paragraph, as amended by G.S.R. 290(E) of 5 May 2020, fixes 7.1% a year for deposits from 1 April 2020.
  • What the texts do not spell out and this site assumes: a month earns one twelfth of the yearly rate, and interest is rounded to the rupee when it is added. Sukanya Samriddhi follows the same pattern in the way we work it; the recurring deposit and time deposit figures compound every quarter, as NSI says for the time deposit; the rule texts of those schemes were not opened. Every result is an estimate and a passbook can differ by a few rupees.
