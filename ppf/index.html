How to choose between them

  • Want income every month or quarter? The Monthly Income Scheme pays monthly (limit ₹9 lakh single, ₹15 lakh joint); the Senior Citizens Savings Scheme pays quarterly (limit ₹30 lakh).
  • Saving for a daughter's future? Sukanya Samriddhi has the highest rate and a 21-year term, with deposits of ₹250 to ₹1,50,000 a year.
  • Long-term, tax-free interest? The PPF has a 15-year term and a ₹1,50,000 yearly limit; its interest is called tax free by the NSI summary.
  • A fixed amount at the end? The NSC, Kisan Vikas Patra and time deposits pay everything at maturity (time deposit interest can be paid out yearly).
  • Small regular saving? The recurring deposit starts at ₹100 a month.

The calculators use the latest rate in the official table, and you can change the rate when a new quarter begins. The comparison above is about the shape of each scheme, not a recommendation.

Common questions

Which small savings scheme has the highest rate?

In the July-September 2026 column of the official table, the Sukanya Samriddhi Account and the Senior Citizens Savings Scheme are highest at 8.2%, followed by the 5-year NSC at 7.7%, the 5-year time deposit at 7.5% and the Kisan Vikas Patra at 7.5%. The schemes differ in who can use them, the deposit limits and the term, so the rate alone does not make one better.

Are the rates fixed?

The government reviews the rates for every quarter. The official table showed no change from April 2024 to July-September 2026, but the October-December 2026 column was still empty on 13 November 2026.

Is this advice?

No. The pages work out arithmetic from published rates and limits and do not recommend a scheme.

  • Minimum and maximum deposits, terms and withdrawal features: the same institute's "National Savings Schemes at a glance" page (nsiindia.gov.in), read on 13 November 2026.
  • PPF interest rule: Public Provident Fund Scheme, 2019, paragraph 7, as published by the National Savings Institute (nsiindia.gov.in), read on 13 November 2026: the lowest balance between the close of the fifth day and the end of the month earns interest, at a rate per annum, and interest is credited at the end of each year. The same paragraph, as amended by G.S.R. 290(E) of 5 May 2020, fixes 7.1% a year for deposits from 1 April 2020.
  • What the texts do not spell out and this site assumes: a month earns one twelfth of the yearly rate, and interest is rounded to the rupee when it is added. Sukanya Samriddhi follows the same pattern in the way we work it; the recurring deposit and time deposit figures compound every quarter, as NSI says for the time deposit; the rule texts of those schemes were not opened. Every result is an estimate and a passbook can differ by a few rupees.
