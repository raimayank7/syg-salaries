SCSS: interest on ₹5,00,000

A Senior Citizen Savings Scheme deposit of ₹5,00,000, at the official rate of 8.2%, pays ₹10,250 every quarter, which is ₹41,000 a year and ₹2,05,000 over the 5-year term. The deposit itself is returned at the end of 5 years.

Income at a glance

Deposit₹5,00,000
Interest each quarter₹10,250
Interest each month, on average₹3,417
Interest each year₹41,000
Interest in 5 years₹2,05,000

What this tells you

  • The scheme pays interest quarterly, so the money arrives four times a year, not every month. If you need a monthly income, the post office Monthly Income Scheme pays monthly, with a lower limit.
  • The most that can be deposited in one account is ₹30 lakh; ₹5,00,000 is within that limit.
  • The NSI summary says the account can be extended on maturity and closed early.
  • The rate of 8.2% is the latest in the official table and is reviewed every quarter; a new deposit takes the rate in force when it is opened, which this site cannot tell you in advance.

Before you invest

Before you deposit, check the eligibility rules and the current rate at the post office or bank, because the NSI summary used here does not list the age conditions. Think about how much of your savings should stay in one place: the scheme has a ceiling of 30 lakh, and the quarterly interest is taxable in the usual way as part of income.

Common questions

How much interest does ₹5,00,000 earn in SCSS?

₹10,250 every quarter at 8.2%, ₹41,000 a year.

Is the interest taxable?

Tax on the interest depends on your total income; this site does not work that out. Check with the Income Tax Department.

  • Minimum and maximum deposits, terms and withdrawal features: the same institute's "National Savings Schemes at a glance" page (nsiindia.gov.in), read on 13 November 2026.
  • PPF interest rule: Public Provident Fund Scheme, 2019, paragraph 7, as published by the National Savings Institute (nsiindia.gov.in), read on 13 November 2026: the lowest balance between the close of the fifth day and the end of the month earns interest, at a rate per annum, and interest is credited at the end of each year. The same paragraph, as amended by G.S.R. 290(E) of 5 May 2020, fixes 7.1% a year for deposits from 1 April 2020.
  • What the texts do not spell out and this site assumes: a month earns one twelfth of the yearly rate, and interest is rounded to the rupee when it is added. Sukanya Samriddhi follows the same pattern in the way we work it; the recurring deposit and time deposit figures compound every quarter, as NSI says for the time deposit; the rule texts of those schemes were not opened. Every result is an estimate and a passbook can differ by a few rupees.
