Reinforcing Iron and Rebar Workers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Reinforcing Iron and Rebar Workers in the United States earned a median wage of $58,970 a year ($28.35 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Position and secure steel bars or mesh in concrete forms in order to reinforce concrete. Use a variety of fasteners, rod-bending machines, blowtorches, and hand tools. Includes rod busters.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 47-2171.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Reinforcing Iron and Rebar Workers is $58,970, which is 16% higher. Among the 60 occupations of the field Construction and Extraction, it ranks number 23 by median pay. With 13,800 jobs, it makes up about 0.9 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($66,060) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Reinforcing Iron and Rebar Workers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Wisconsin ($121,620), New Jersey ($116,110) and Washington ($108,970). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($27,120), Guam ($36,830) and Mississippi ($39,860). The most jobs were in Texas (3,640), California (2,390) and Florida (1,150). The occupation is most concentrated in Guam, where its share of all jobs is 67.8 times the national share.
