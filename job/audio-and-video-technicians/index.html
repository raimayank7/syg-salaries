Audio and Video Technicians: pay in the United States, May 2025

Audio and Video Technicians in the United States earned a median wage of $58,100 a year ($27.93 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Set up, maintain, and dismantle audio and video equipment, such as microphones, sound speakers, connecting wires and cables, sound and mixing boards, video cameras, video monitors and servers, and related electronic equipment for live or recorded events, such as concerts, meetings, conventions, presentations, podcasts, news conferences, and sporting events. Excludes “Sound Engineering Technicians” (27-4014), “Lighting Technicians” (27-4015), and “Audiovisual Equipment Installers and Repairers” (49-2097).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 27-4011.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Audio and Video Technicians is $58,100, which is 14% higher. Among the 41 occupations of the field Arts, Design, Entertainment, Sports, and Media, it ranks number 29 by median pay. With 70,230 jobs, it makes up about 4.5 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($64,630) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Audio and Video Technicians pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($78,770), New Jersey ($71,040) and Maryland ($70,040). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($30,860), Mississippi ($43,500) and Arkansas ($43,910). The most jobs were in California (9,280), New York (7,990) and Florida (6,000). The occupation is most concentrated in District of Columbia, where its share of all jobs is 2.6 times the national share.
