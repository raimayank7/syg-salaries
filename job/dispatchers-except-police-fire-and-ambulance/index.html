Dispatchers, Except Police, Fire, and Ambulance: pay in the United States, May 2025

Dispatchers, Except Police, Fire, and Ambulance in the United States earned a median wage of $50,340 a year ($24.20 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Schedule and dispatch workers, work crews, equipment, or service vehicles for conveyance of materials, freight, or passengers, or for normal installation, service, or emergency repairs rendered outside the place of business. Duties may include using radio, telephone, or computer to transmit assignments and compiling statistics and reports on work progress.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 43-5032.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Dispatchers, Except Police, Fire, and Ambulance is $50,340, which is about the same. Among the 54 occupations of the field Office and Administrative Support, it ranks number 20 by median pay. With 202,810 jobs, it makes up about 13 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Dispatchers, Except Police, Fire, and Ambulance pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($72,260), Washington ($59,330) and Minnesota ($59,220). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($24,750), Virgin Islands ($33,870) and Guam ($39,630). The most jobs were in California (23,970), Texas (23,920) and Florida (12,550). The occupation is most concentrated in Guam, where its share of all jobs is 1.5 times the national share.
