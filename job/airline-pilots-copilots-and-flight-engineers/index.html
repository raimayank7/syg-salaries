Airline Pilots, Copilots, and Flight Engineers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Airline Pilots, Copilots, and Flight Engineers in the United States earned a median wage of $232,140 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Pilot and navigate the flight of fixed-wing aircraft, usually on scheduled air carrier routes, for the transport of passengers and cargo. Requires Federal Air Transport certificate and rating for specific aircraft type used. Includes regional, national, and international airline pilots and flight instructors of airline pilots. Excludes “Electro-Mechanical and Mechatronics Technologists and Technicians” (17-3024).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 53-2011.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Airline Pilots, Copilots, and Flight Engineers is $232,140, which is 355% higher. Among the 50 occupations of the field Transportation and Material Moving, it ranks number 1 by median pay. With 103,560 jobs, it makes up about 6.7 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($288,650) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Airline Pilots, Copilots, and Flight Engineers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Georgia ($618,090), Idaho ($507,640) and Michigan ($428,570). The lowest was in Missouri ($97,680), New Hampshire ($99,280) and Louisiana ($120,740). The most jobs were in Texas (10,930), California (10,670) and Georgia (8,770). The occupation is most concentrated in Alaska, where its share of all jobs is 6.2 times the national share.
