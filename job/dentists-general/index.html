Dentists, General: pay in the United States, May 2025

Dentists, General in the United States earned a median wage of $170,950 a year ($82.19 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Examine, diagnose, and treat diseases, injuries, and malformations of teeth and gums. May treat diseases of nerve, pulp, and other dental tissues affecting oral hygiene and retention of teeth. May fit dental appliances or provide preventive care. Excludes “Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons” (29-1022), “Orthodontists” (29-1023), “Prosthodontists” (29-1024), and “Dentists, All Other Specialists” (29-1029).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-1021.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Dentists, General is $170,950, which is 235% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 23 by median pay. With 124,390 jobs, it makes up about 8.0 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($191,350) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Dentists, General pay by state

The highest median pay was in Alaska ($230,990), North Dakota ($220,660) and Minnesota ($219,200). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($107,560), Wyoming ($123,830) and Idaho ($124,190). The most jobs were in California (16,770), Florida (9,320) and Texas (8,920). The occupation is most concentrated in Hawaii, where its share of all jobs is 1.7 times the national share.
