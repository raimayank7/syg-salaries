Solar Photovoltaic Installers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Solar Photovoltaic Installers in the United States earned a median wage of $53,140 a year ($25.55 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Assemble, install, or maintain solar photovoltaic (PV) systems on roofs or other structures in compliance with site assessment and schematics. May include measuring, cutting, assembling, and bolting structural framing and solar modules. May perform minor electrical work such as current checks. Excludes solar PV electricians who are included in “Electricians” (47-2111) and solar thermal installers who are included in “Plumbers, Pipefitters, and Steamfitters” (47-2152).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 47-2231.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Solar Photovoltaic Installers is $53,140, which is 4% higher. Among the 60 occupations of the field Construction and Extraction, it ranks number 38 by median pay. With 31,350 jobs, it makes up about 2.0 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Solar Photovoltaic Installers pay by state

The highest median pay was in New Jersey ($78,950), Hawaii ($70,370) and Nevada ($67,810). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($26,860), South Dakota ($42,380) and North Carolina ($43,860). The most jobs were in California (6,830), Texas (5,270) and Florida (2,980). The occupation is most concentrated in Puerto Rico, where its share of all jobs is 10.0 times the national share.
