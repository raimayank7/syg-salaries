Forest Fire Inspectors and Prevention Specialists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Forest Fire Inspectors and Prevention Specialists in the United States earned a median wage of $56,870 a year ($27.34 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Enforce fire regulations, inspect forest for fire hazards, and recommend forest fire prevention or control measures. May report forest fires and weather conditions.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 33-2022.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Forest Fire Inspectors and Prevention Specialists is $56,870, which is 12% higher. Among the 24 occupations of the field Protective Service, it ranks number 13 by median pay. With 2,780 jobs, it makes up about 0.2 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($66,640) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Forest Fire Inspectors and Prevention Specialists pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($97,370), Utah ($90,020) and Louisiana ($75,480). The lowest was in Mississippi ($31,720), Alabama ($37,430) and Arkansas ($43,310). The most jobs were in California (480), Georgia (350) and Oklahoma (310). The occupation is most concentrated in Oklahoma, where its share of all jobs is 10.2 times the national share.
