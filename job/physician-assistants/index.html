Physician Assistants: pay in the United States, May 2025

Physician Assistants in the United States earned a median wage of $135,880 a year ($65.33 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Provide healthcare services typically performed by a physician, under the supervision of a physician. Conduct complete physicals, provide treatment, and counsel patients. May, in some cases, prescribe medication. Must graduate from an accredited educational program for physician assistants. Excludes “Registered Nurses” (29-1141), “Nurse Anesthetists” (29-1151), “Nurse Midwives” (29-1161), “Nurse Practitioners” (29-1171), “Emergency Medical Technicians” (29-2042), “Paramedics” (29-2043), “Surgical Assistants” (29-9093), and “Medical Assistants” (31-9092).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-1071.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Physician Assistants is $135,880, which is 167% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 28 by median pay. With 162,150 jobs, it makes up about 10 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Physician Assistants pay by state

The highest median pay was in New Jersey ($165,690), California ($165,650) and Washington ($164,360). The lowest was in Alabama ($104,960), Mississippi ($105,920) and Georgia ($115,390). The most jobs were in New York (19,140), California (13,600) and Texas (10,110). The occupation is most concentrated in Alaska, where its share of all jobs is 2.1 times the national share.
