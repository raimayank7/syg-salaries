Health Specialties Teachers, Postsecondary: pay in the United States, May 2025

Health Specialties Teachers, Postsecondary in the United States earned a median wage of $107,310 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Teach courses in health specialties, in fields such as dentistry, laboratory technology, medicine, pharmacy, public health, therapy, and veterinary medicine. Excludes “Biological Science Teachers, Postsecondary” (25-1042) and “Nursing Instructors and Teachers, Postsecondary” (25-1072) who teach medical science.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-1071.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Health Specialties Teachers, Postsecondary is $107,310, which is 110% higher. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 4 by median pay. With 221,270 jobs, it makes up about 14 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($147,570) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Health Specialties Teachers, Postsecondary pay by state

The highest median pay was in Utah ($168,060), District of Columbia ($167,520) and California ($165,110). The lowest was in West Virginia ($60,300), Alaska ($73,890) and Ohio ($74,720). The most jobs were in New York (21,410), Texas (18,960) and California (17,350). The occupation is most concentrated in Connecticut, where its share of all jobs is 3.0 times the national share.
