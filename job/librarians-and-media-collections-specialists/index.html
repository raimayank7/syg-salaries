Librarians and Media Collections Specialists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Librarians and Media Collections Specialists in the United States earned a median wage of $68,270 a year ($32.82 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Administer and maintain libraries or collections of information, for public or private access through reference or borrowing. Work in a variety of settings, such as educational institutions, museums, and corporations, and with various types of informational materials, such as books, periodicals, recordings, films, and databases. Tasks may include acquiring, cataloging, and circulating library materials, and user services such as locating and organizing information, providing instruction on how to access information, and setting up and operating a library’s media equipment.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-4022.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Librarians and Media Collections Specialists is $68,270, which is 34% higher. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 41 by median pay. With 133,790 jobs, it makes up about 8.6 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Librarians and Media Collections Specialists pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($100,030), District of Columbia ($96,550) and California ($89,790). The lowest was in West Virginia ($42,980), Wyoming ($46,530) and South Dakota ($51,210). The most jobs were in New York (11,870), California (10,180) and Texas (9,150). The occupation is most concentrated in Vermont, where its share of all jobs is 2.3 times the national share.
