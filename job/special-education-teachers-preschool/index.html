Special Education Teachers, Preschool: pay in the United States, May 2025

Special Education Teachers, Preschool in the United States earned a median wage of $64,830 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Teach academic, social, and life skills to preschool-aged students with learning, emotional, or physical disabilities. Includes teachers who specialize and work with students who are blind or have visual impairments; students who are deaf or have hearing impairments; and students with intellectual disabilities. Excludes “Substitute Teachers, Short-Term” (25-3031).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-2051.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Special Education Teachers, Preschool is $64,830, which is 27% higher. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 47 by median pay. With 29,510 jobs, it makes up about 1.9 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($76,380) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Special Education Teachers, Preschool pay by state

The highest median pay was in Massachusetts ($99,070), New Jersey ($92,060) and Connecticut ($87,960). The lowest was in Arkansas ($35,580), Indiana ($48,680) and Idaho ($49,610). The most jobs were in New York (6,220), California (2,510) and Texas (2,010). The occupation is most concentrated in New York, where its share of all jobs is 3.4 times the national share.
