First-Line Supervisors of Security Workers: pay in the United States, May 2025

First-Line Supervisors of Security Workers in the United States earned a median wage of $55,940 a year ($26.90 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for First-Line Supervisors of Security Workers is $55,940, which is 10% higher. Among the 24 occupations of the field Protective Service, it ranks number 15 by median pay. With 81,480 jobs, it makes up about 5.2 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

First-Line Supervisors of Security Workers pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($83,710), Vermont ($78,110) and Alaska ($73,500). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($22,640), Guam ($42,550) and Mississippi ($43,410). The most jobs were in California (10,850), New York (7,050) and Florida (6,560). The occupation is most concentrated in Virgin Islands, where its share of all jobs is 3.9 times the national share.
