Stockers and Order Fillers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Stockers and Order Fillers in the United States earned a median wage of $37,330 a year ($17.95 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Receive, store, and issue merchandise, materials, equipment, and other items from stockroom, warehouse, or storage yard to fill shelves, racks, tables, or customers’ orders. May operate power equipment to fill orders. May mark prices on merchandise and set up sales displays. Excludes “Shipping, Receiving, and Inventory Clerks” (43-5071), “Laborers and Freight, Stock, and Material Movers, Hand” (53-7062), and “Packers and Packagers, Hand” (53-7064).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 53-7065.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Stockers and Order Fillers is $37,330, which is 27% lower. Among the 50 occupations of the field Transportation and Material Moving, it ranks number 43 by median pay. With 2,833,810 jobs, it makes up about 182 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Stockers and Order Fillers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($44,620), Oregon ($43,480) and California ($42,870). The lowest was in Guam ($22,910), Puerto Rico ($25,030) and Virgin Islands ($28,130). The most jobs were in Texas (322,710), California (310,870) and Florida (171,380). The occupation is most concentrated in South Dakota, where its share of all jobs is 1.4 times the national share.
