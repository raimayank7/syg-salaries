Helpers--Extraction Workers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Helpers--Extraction Workers in the United States earned a median wage of $47,730 a year ($22.95 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Help extraction craft workers, such as earth drillers, blasters and explosives workers, derrick operators, and mining machine operators, by performing duties requiring less skill. Duties include supplying equipment or cleaning work area. Apprentice workers are classified with the appropriate skilled construction trade occupation (47-2011 through 47-2231).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 47-5081.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Helpers--Extraction Workers is $47,730, which is 6% lower. Among the 60 occupations of the field Construction and Extraction, it ranks number 51 by median pay. With 6,700 jobs, it makes up about 0.4 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Helpers--Extraction Workers pay by state

The highest median pay was in West Virginia ($77,180), Kentucky ($68,020) and Utah ($55,490). The lowest was in Mississippi ($36,160), Colorado ($37,450) and Oklahoma ($38,510). The most jobs were in California (1,170), Texas (970) and Florida (420). The occupation is most concentrated in West Virginia, where its share of all jobs is 11.9 times the national share.
