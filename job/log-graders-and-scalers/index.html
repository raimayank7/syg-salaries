Log Graders and Scalers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Log Graders and Scalers in the United States earned a median wage of $46,330 a year ($22.27 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Grade logs or estimate the marketable content or value of logs or pulpwood in sorting yards, millpond, log deck, or similar locations. Inspect logs for defects or measure logs to determine volume. Excludes “Buyers and Purchasing Agents, Farm Products” (13-1021).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 45-4023.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Log Graders and Scalers is $46,330, which is 9% lower. Among the 13 occupations of the field Farming, Fishing, and Forestry, it ranks number 7 by median pay. With 3,070 jobs, it makes up about 0.2 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Log Graders and Scalers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($61,580), Oregon ($60,250) and Idaho ($57,620). The lowest was in Tennessee ($35,920), Texas ($36,100) and Virginia ($36,220). The most jobs were in Pennsylvania (330), California (320) and North Carolina (200). The occupation is most concentrated in Maine, where its share of all jobs is 9.5 times the national share.
