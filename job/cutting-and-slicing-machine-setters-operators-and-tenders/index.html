Cutting and Slicing Machine Setters, Operators, and Tenders: pay in the United States, May 2025

Cutting and Slicing Machine Setters, Operators, and Tenders in the United States earned a median wage of $46,570 a year ($22.39 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Set up, operate, or tend machines that cut or slice materials, such as glass, stone, cork, rubber, tobacco, food, paper, or insulating material. Excludes “Cutting, Punching, and Press Machine Setters, Operators, and Tenders, Metal and Plastic” (51-4031), “Textile Cutting Machine Setters, Operators, and Tenders” (51-6062), and “Woodworking Machine Setters, Operators, and Tenders” (51-7040).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 51-9032.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Cutting and Slicing Machine Setters, Operators, and Tenders is $46,570, which is 9% lower. Among the 105 occupations of the field Production, it ranks number 56 by median pay. With 44,980 jobs, it makes up about 2.9 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Cutting and Slicing Machine Setters, Operators, and Tenders pay by state

The highest median pay was in Minnesota ($58,260), Vermont ($52,220) and Colorado ($51,750). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($25,010), New Mexico ($32,770) and Oklahoma ($37,910). The most jobs were in Texas (3,650), North Carolina (3,100) and California (3,080). The occupation is most concentrated in South Dakota, where its share of all jobs is 3.5 times the national share.
