Criminal Justice and Law Enforcement Teachers, Postsecondary: pay in the United States, May 2025

Criminal Justice and Law Enforcement Teachers, Postsecondary in the United States earned a median wage of $76,590 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Teach courses in criminal justice, corrections, and law enforcement administration. Includes both teachers primarily engaged in teaching and those who do a combination of teaching and research.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-1111.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Criminal Justice and Law Enforcement Teachers, Postsecondary is $76,590, which is 50% higher. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 34 by median pay. With 13,150 jobs, it makes up about 0.8 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($87,550) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Criminal Justice and Law Enforcement Teachers, Postsecondary pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($139,720), Colorado ($122,050) and Maryland ($99,600). The lowest was in Kansas ($50,270), Kentucky ($60,280) and Florida ($61,550). The most jobs were in California (1,460), North Carolina (1,220) and Texas (1,150). The occupation is most concentrated in North Carolina, where its share of all jobs is 2.9 times the national share.
