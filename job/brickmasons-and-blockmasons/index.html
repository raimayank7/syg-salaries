Brickmasons and Blockmasons: pay in the United States, May 2025

Brickmasons and Blockmasons in the United States earned a median wage of $62,120 a year ($29.87 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Lay and bind building materials, such as brick, structural tile, concrete block, cinder block, glass block, and terra-cotta block, with mortar and other substances, to construct or repair walls, partitions, arches, sewers, and other structures. Installers of mortarless segmental concrete masonry wall units are classified in “Landscaping and Groundskeeping Workers” (37-3011). Excludes “Stonemasons” (47-2022).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 47-2021.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Brickmasons and Blockmasons is $62,120, which is 22% higher. Among the 60 occupations of the field Construction and Extraction, it ranks number 16 by median pay. With 52,550 jobs, it makes up about 3.4 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Brickmasons and Blockmasons pay by state

The highest median pay was in Minnesota ($95,220), Massachusetts ($94,950) and Illinois ($89,980). The lowest was in Maine ($42,710), New Mexico ($44,980) and West Virginia ($46,600). The most jobs were in Texas (4,300), California (3,820) and New York (3,790). The occupation is most concentrated in Virgin Islands, where its share of all jobs is 8.3 times the national share.
