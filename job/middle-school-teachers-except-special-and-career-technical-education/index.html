Middle School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education: pay in the United States, May 2025

Middle School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education in the United States earned a median wage of $64,370 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Teach one or more subjects to students at the middle, intermediate, or junior high school level. Excludes “Career/Technical Education Teachers, Middle School” (25-2023), “Special Education Teachers” (25-2050), and “Substitute Teachers, Short Term” (25-3031).

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Middle School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education is $64,370, which is 26% higher. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 49 by median pay. With 620,090 jobs, it makes up about 40 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($72,030) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Middle School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($102,140), California ($98,950) and New York ($94,840). The lowest was in Oklahoma ($48,440), Mississippi ($50,790) and South Dakota ($50,980). The most jobs were in Texas (80,160), New York (38,670) and California (37,300). The occupation is most concentrated in Guam, where its share of all jobs is 1.9 times the national share.
