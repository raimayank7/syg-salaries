Continuous Mining Machine Operators: pay in the United States, May 2025

Continuous Mining Machine Operators in the United States earned a median wage of $61,810 a year ($29.72 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Operate self-propelled mining machines that rip coal, metal and nonmetal ores, rock, stone, or sand from the mine face and load it onto conveyors, shuttle cars, or trucks in a continuous operation.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 47-5041.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Continuous Mining Machine Operators is $61,810, which is 21% higher. Among the 60 occupations of the field Construction and Extraction, it ranks number 17 by median pay. With 14,000 jobs, it makes up about 0.9 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Continuous Mining Machine Operators pay by state

The highest median pay was in Minnesota ($90,820), Nevada ($85,590) and Wyoming ($85,260). The lowest was in New York ($46,450), Oklahoma ($46,980) and Missouri ($47,280). The most jobs were in Arizona (2,850), Nevada (1,110) and California (940). The occupation is most concentrated in Wyoming, where its share of all jobs is 13.0 times the national share.
