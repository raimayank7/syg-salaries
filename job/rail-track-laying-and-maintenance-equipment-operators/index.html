Rail-Track Laying and Maintenance Equipment Operators: pay in the United States, May 2025

Rail-Track Laying and Maintenance Equipment Operators in the United States earned a median wage of $70,070 a year ($33.69 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Lay, repair, and maintain track for standard or narrow-gauge railroad equipment used in regular railroad service or in plant yards, quarries, sand and gravel pits, and mines. Includes ballast cleaning machine operators and railroad bed tamping machine operators.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 47-4061.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Rail-Track Laying and Maintenance Equipment Operators is $70,070, which is 37% higher. Among the 60 occupations of the field Construction and Extraction, it ranks number 10 by median pay. With 19,580 jobs, it makes up about 1.3 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Rail-Track Laying and Maintenance Equipment Operators pay by state

The highest median pay was in Massachusetts ($94,330), Maryland ($92,030) and New York ($87,150). The lowest was in Louisiana ($44,150), Iowa ($45,640) and North Carolina ($47,700). The most jobs were in New York (3,590), Illinois (1,190) and Ohio (1,130). The occupation is most concentrated in Wyoming, where its share of all jobs is 6.3 times the national share.
