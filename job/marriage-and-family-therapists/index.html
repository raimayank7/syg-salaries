Marriage and Family Therapists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Marriage and Family Therapists in the United States earned a median wage of $66,940 a year ($32.18 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Diagnose and treat mental and emotional disorders, whether cognitive, affective, or behavioral, within the context of marriage and family systems. Apply psychotherapeutic and family systems theories and techniques in the delivery of services to individuals, couples, and families for the purpose of treating such diagnosed nervous and mental disorders. Excludes “Psychologists” (19-3032 through 19-3039) and “Social Workers” (21-1020).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 21-1013.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Marriage and Family Therapists is $66,940, which is 31% higher. Among the 17 occupations of the field Community and Social Service, it ranks number 3 by median pay. With 66,740 jobs, it makes up about 4.3 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($76,960) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Marriage and Family Therapists pay by state

The highest median pay was in Hawaii ($141,960), New Jersey ($92,150) and Georgia ($91,550). The lowest was in Wisconsin ($37,870), South Dakota ($44,870) and Texas ($46,300). The most jobs were in California (34,970), Minnesota (4,870) and New Jersey (4,040). The occupation is most concentrated in California, where its share of all jobs is 4.5 times the national share.
