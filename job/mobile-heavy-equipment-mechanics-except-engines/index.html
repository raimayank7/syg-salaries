Mobile Heavy Equipment Mechanics, Except Engines: pay in the United States, May 2025

Mobile Heavy Equipment Mechanics, Except Engines in the United States earned a median wage of $65,510 a year ($31.50 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Diagnose, adjust, repair, or overhaul mobile mechanical, hydraulic, and pneumatic equipment, such as cranes, bulldozers, graders, and conveyors, used in construction, logging, and mining. Excludes “Bus and Truck Mechanics and Diesel Engine Specialists” (49-3031) and “Rail Car Repairers” (49-3043).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 49-3042.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Mobile Heavy Equipment Mechanics, Except Engines is $65,510, which is 29% higher. Among the 51 occupations of the field Installation, Maintenance, and Repair, it ranks number 16 by median pay. With 176,600 jobs, it makes up about 11 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Mobile Heavy Equipment Mechanics, Except Engines pay by state

The highest median pay was in Alaska ($82,670), Nevada ($79,860) and Minnesota ($79,370). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($43,510), Guam ($45,280) and West Virginia ($56,810). The most jobs were in California (20,080), Texas (20,080) and Florida (9,240). The occupation is most concentrated in Wyoming, where its share of all jobs is 4.4 times the national share.
