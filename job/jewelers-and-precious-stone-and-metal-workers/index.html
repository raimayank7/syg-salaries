Jewelers and Precious Stone and Metal Workers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Jewelers and Precious Stone and Metal Workers in the United States earned a median wage of $52,540 a year ($25.26 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Design, fabricate, adjust, repair, or appraise jewelry, gold, silver, other precious metals, or gems. Includes diamond polishers and gem cutters, and persons who perform precision casting and modeling of molds, casting metal in molds, or setting precious and semiprecious stones for jewelry and related products.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 51-9071.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Jewelers and Precious Stone and Metal Workers is $52,540, which is 3% higher. Among the 105 occupations of the field Production, it ranks number 27 by median pay. With 22,440 jobs, it makes up about 1.4 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($57,960) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Jewelers and Precious Stone and Metal Workers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Connecticut ($69,800), Virginia ($61,940) and New York ($61,720). The lowest was in Arkansas ($35,950), Montana ($37,580) and Utah ($39,860). The most jobs were in New York (4,300), California (2,300) and Texas (1,810). The occupation is most concentrated in Rhode Island, where its share of all jobs is 7.5 times the national share.
