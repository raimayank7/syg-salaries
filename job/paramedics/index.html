Paramedics: pay in the United States, May 2025

Paramedics in the United States earned a median wage of $60,600 a year ($29.13 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Administer basic or advanced emergency medical care and assess injuries and illnesses. May administer medication intravenously, use equipment such as EKGs, or administer advanced life support to sick or injured individuals. Excludes “Emergency Medical Technicians” (29-2042) and “Ambulance Drivers and Attendants, Except Emergency Medical Technicians” (53-3011).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-2043.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Paramedics is $60,600, which is 19% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 61 by median pay. With 100,610 jobs, it makes up about 6.5 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Paramedics pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($104,310), New Jersey ($86,630) and Hawaii ($81,280). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($29,960), Oklahoma ($46,790) and West Virginia ($48,460). The most jobs were in Texas (8,410), Florida (6,970) and New York (6,110). The occupation is most concentrated in West Virginia, where its share of all jobs is 2.4 times the national share.
