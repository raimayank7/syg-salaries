Aerospace Engineering and Operations Technologists and Technicians: pay in the United States, May 2025

Aerospace Engineering and Operations Technologists and Technicians in the United States earned a median wage of $82,890 a year ($39.85 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Operate, install, adjust, and maintain integrated computer/communications systems, consoles, simulators, and other data acquisition, test, and measurement instruments and equipment, which are used to launch, track, position, and evaluate air and space vehicles. May record and interpret test data.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 17-3021.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Aerospace Engineering and Operations Technologists and Technicians is $82,890, which is 63% higher. Among the 36 occupations of the field Architecture and Engineering, it ranks number 20 by median pay. With 11,280 jobs, it makes up about 0.7 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($91,310) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Aerospace Engineering and Operations Technologists and Technicians pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($125,160), Alaska ($105,780) and Nevada ($102,980). The lowest was in Oklahoma ($45,590), Pennsylvania ($60,770) and Alabama ($62,130). The most jobs were in Washington (1,590), California (1,560) and Texas (950). The occupation is most concentrated in Washington, where its share of all jobs is 6.2 times the national share.
