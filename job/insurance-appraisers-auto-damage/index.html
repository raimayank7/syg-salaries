What the occupation covers

Appraise automobile or other vehicle damage to determine repair costs for insurance claim settlement. Prepare insurance forms to indicate repair cost or cost estimates and recommendations. May seek agreement with automotive repair shop on repair costs.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 13-1032.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Insurance Appraisers, Auto Damage is $78,240, which is 53% higher. Among the 32 occupations of the field Business and Financial Operations, it ranks number 19 by median pay. With 11,560 jobs, it makes up about 0.7 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Insurance Appraisers, Auto Damage pay by state

The highest median pay was in Kentucky ($97,950), Pennsylvania ($95,720) and New Jersey ($94,280). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($46,520), Florida ($61,320) and Nebraska ($62,350). The most jobs were in Texas (910), Ohio (820) and Massachusetts (680). The occupation is most concentrated in Puerto Rico, where its share of all jobs is 4.3 times the national share.
