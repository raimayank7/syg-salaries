Law Teachers, Postsecondary: pay in the United States, May 2025

Law Teachers, Postsecondary in the United States earned a median wage of $128,500 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Teach courses in law. Includes both teachers primarily engaged in teaching and those who do a combination of teaching and research.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-1112.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Law Teachers, Postsecondary is $128,500, which is 152% higher. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 1 by median pay. With 20,060 jobs, it makes up about 1.3 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($144,770) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Law Teachers, Postsecondary pay by state

The highest median pay was in Minnesota ($171,790), Oregon ($167,010) and South Carolina ($162,400). The lowest was in Utah ($68,270), Mississippi ($74,440) and Ohio ($75,930). The most jobs were in California (7,800), New York (2,190) and Texas (880). The occupation is most concentrated in District of Columbia, where its share of all jobs is 7.4 times the national share.
