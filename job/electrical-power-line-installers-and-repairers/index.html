Electrical Power-Line Installers and Repairers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Electrical Power-Line Installers and Repairers in the United States earned a median wage of $95,320 a year ($45.83 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Install or repair cables or wires used in electrical power or distribution systems. May erect poles and light or heavy duty transmission towers. Excludes “Electrical and Electronics Repairers, Powerhouse, Substation, and Relay” (49-2095).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 49-9051.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Electrical Power-Line Installers and Repairers is $95,320, which is 87% higher. Among the 51 occupations of the field Installation, Maintenance, and Repair, it ranks number 2 by median pay. With 131,070 jobs, it makes up about 8.4 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Electrical Power-Line Installers and Repairers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Hawaii ($134,340), Washington ($133,060) and Oregon ($131,090). The lowest was in Guam ($64,650), Arizona ($75,420) and Mississippi ($75,890). The most jobs were in Texas (17,280), California (8,930) and Florida (6,420). The occupation is most concentrated in Wyoming, where its share of all jobs is 2.8 times the national share.
