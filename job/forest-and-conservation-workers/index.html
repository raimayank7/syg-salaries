Forest and Conservation Workers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Forest and Conservation Workers in the United States earned a median wage of $43,680 a year ($21.00 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Under supervision, perform manual labor necessary to develop, maintain, or protect areas such as forests, forested areas, woodlands, wetlands, and rangelands through such activities as raising and transporting seedlings; combating insects, pests, and diseases harmful to plant life; and building structures to control water, erosion, and leaching of soil. Includes forester aides, seedling pullers, tree planters, and gatherers of nontimber forestry products such as pine straw.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 45-4011.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Forest and Conservation Workers is $43,680, which is 14% lower. Among the 13 occupations of the field Farming, Fishing, and Forestry, it ranks number 8 by median pay. With 6,050 jobs, it makes up about 0.4 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Forest and Conservation Workers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Idaho ($59,460), Hawaii ($56,330) and Virginia ($54,560). The lowest was in Texas ($30,470), Montana ($32,600) and Iowa ($33,600). The most jobs were in California (2,270), South Dakota (490) and Washington (460). The occupation is most concentrated in South Dakota, where its share of all jobs is 27.5 times the national share.
