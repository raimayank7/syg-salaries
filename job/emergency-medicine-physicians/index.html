Emergency Medicine Physicians: pay in the United States, May 2025

Emergency Medicine Physicians in the United States earned a median wage of $335,550 a year ($161.32 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Make immediate medical decisions and act to prevent death or further disability. Provide immediate recognition, evaluation, care, stabilization, and disposition of patients. May direct emergency medical staff in an emergency department.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-1214.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Emergency Medicine Physicians is $335,550, which is 558% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 8 by median pay. With 32,880 jobs, it makes up about 2.1 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Emergency Medicine Physicians pay by state

The highest median pay was in Rhode Island ($512,730), West Virginia ($491,230) and Missouri ($456,280). The lowest was in Utah ($140,370), Kentucky ($169,390) and Florida ($204,990). The most jobs were in California (4,420), New York (3,980) and Florida (2,060). The occupation is most concentrated in Alaska, where its share of all jobs is 2.8 times the national share.
