Physical Therapists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Physical Therapists in the United States earned a median wage of $102,760 a year ($49.40 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Assess, plan, organize, and participate in rehabilitative programs that improve mobility, relieve pain, increase strength, and improve or correct disabling conditions resulting from disease or injury.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Physical Therapists is $102,760, which is 102% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 34 by median pay. With 267,330 jobs, it makes up about 17 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Physical Therapists pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($124,190), Alaska ($115,280) and New Jersey ($112,140). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($60,250), North Dakota ($85,330) and Montana ($91,450). The most jobs were in California (26,580), Texas (21,870) and New York (20,940). The occupation is most concentrated in Maine, where its share of all jobs is 1.7 times the national share.
