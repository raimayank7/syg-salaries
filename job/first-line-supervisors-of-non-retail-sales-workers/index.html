First-Line Supervisors of Non-Retail Sales Workers: pay in the United States, May 2025

First-Line Supervisors of Non-Retail Sales Workers in the United States earned a median wage of $87,520 a year ($42.08 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Directly supervise and coordinate activities of sales workers other than retail sales workers. May perform duties such as budgeting, accounting, and personnel work, in addition to supervisory duties.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 41-1012.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for First-Line Supervisors of Non-Retail Sales Workers is $87,520, which is 72% higher. Among the 22 occupations of the field Sales and Related, it ranks number 3 by median pay. With 214,390 jobs, it makes up about 14 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($99,370) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

First-Line Supervisors of Non-Retail Sales Workers pay by state

The highest median pay was in New York ($121,780), Massachusetts ($115,150) and Colorado ($110,450). The lowest was in Guam ($45,900), Puerto Rico ($46,260) and West Virginia ($62,970). The most jobs were in Texas (20,690), California (19,600) and New York (16,870). The occupation is most concentrated in Colorado, where its share of all jobs is 2.1 times the national share.
