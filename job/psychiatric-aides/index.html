Psychiatric Aides: pay in the United States, May 2025

Psychiatric Aides in the United States earned a median wage of $44,910 a year ($21.59 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Assist mentally impaired or emotionally disturbed patients, working under direction of nursing and medical staff. May assist with daily living activities, lead patients in educational and recreational activities, or accompany patients to and from examinations and treatments. May restrain violent patients. Includes psychiatric orderlies.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 31-1133.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Psychiatric Aides is $44,910, which is 12% lower. Among the 17 occupations of the field Healthcare Support, it ranks number 9 by median pay. With 35,520 jobs, it makes up about 2.3 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Psychiatric Aides pay by state

The highest median pay was in Minnesota ($55,040), Washington ($52,570) and Hawaii ($52,310). The lowest was in Alabama ($27,150), Louisiana ($30,030) and Mississippi ($30,510). The most jobs were in New York (5,120), California (3,760) and Mississippi (2,490). The occupation is most concentrated in Mississippi, where its share of all jobs is 9.4 times the national share.
