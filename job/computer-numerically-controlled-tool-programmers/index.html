Computer Numerically Controlled Tool Programmers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Computer Numerically Controlled Tool Programmers in the United States earned a median wage of $68,120 a year ($32.75 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Develop programs to control machining or processing of materials by automatic machine tools, equipment, or systems. May also set up, operate, or maintain equipment.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 51-9162.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Computer Numerically Controlled Tool Programmers is $68,120, which is 34% higher. Among the 105 occupations of the field Production, it ranks number 9 by median pay. With 28,500 jobs, it makes up about 1.8 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Computer Numerically Controlled Tool Programmers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Connecticut ($86,390), California ($82,090) and Massachusetts ($81,670). The lowest was in Mississippi ($50,190), Arkansas ($56,390) and Nevada ($57,210). The most jobs were in California (2,990), Ohio (2,370) and Michigan (2,300). The occupation is most concentrated in Wisconsin, where its share of all jobs is 3.0 times the national share.
