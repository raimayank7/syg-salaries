Surveyors: pay in the United States, May 2025

Surveyors in the United States earned a median wage of $75,440 a year ($36.27 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Make exact measurements and determine property boundaries. Provide data relevant to the shape, contour, gravitation, location, elevation, or dimension of land or land features on or near the earth’s surface for engineering, mapmaking, mining, land evaluation, construction, and other purposes.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 17-1022.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Surveyors is $75,440, which is 48% higher. Among the 36 occupations of the field Architecture and Engineering, it ranks number 26 by median pay. With 50,830 jobs, it makes up about 3.3 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Surveyors pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($105,330), Alaska ($99,550) and Oregon ($97,070). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($44,870), Arkansas ($51,800) and Georgia ($58,100). The most jobs were in Texas (6,410), California (4,520) and Florida (4,000). The occupation is most concentrated in West Virginia, where its share of all jobs is 2.8 times the national share.
