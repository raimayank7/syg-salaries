Child, Family, and School Social Workers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Child, Family, and School Social Workers in the United States earned a median wage of $59,550 a year ($28.63 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Provide social services and assistance to improve the social and psychological functioning of children and their families and to maximize the family well-being and the academic functioning of children. May assist parents, arrange adoptions, and find foster homes for abandoned or abused children. In schools, they address such problems as teenage pregnancy, misbehavior, and truancy. May also advise teachers.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 21-1021.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Child, Family, and School Social Workers is $59,550, which is 17% higher. Among the 17 occupations of the field Community and Social Service, it ranks number 9 by median pay. With 392,550 jobs, it makes up about 25 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Child, Family, and School Social Workers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Connecticut ($78,970), Maryland ($76,390) and New Jersey ($76,280). The lowest was in Arkansas ($43,330), Puerto Rico ($43,620) and Oklahoma ($43,950). The most jobs were in California (60,310), New York (30,170) and Texas (21,670). The occupation is most concentrated in Rhode Island, where its share of all jobs is 1.9 times the national share.
