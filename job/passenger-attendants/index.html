Passenger Attendants: pay in the United States, May 2025

Passenger Attendants in the United States earned a median wage of $37,720 a year ($18.14 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Provide services to ensure the safety of passengers aboard ships, buses, trains, or within the station or terminal. Perform duties such as explaining the use of safety equipment, serving meals or beverages, or answering questions related to travel. Excludes “Baggage Porters and Bellhops” (39-6011) and “Flight Attendants” (53-2031).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 53-6061.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Passenger Attendants is $37,720, which is 26% lower. Among the 50 occupations of the field Transportation and Material Moving, it ranks number 42 by median pay. With 27,110 jobs, it makes up about 1.7 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Passenger Attendants pay by state

The highest median pay was in Alaska ($54,290), District of Columbia ($52,730) and Kentucky ($44,810). The lowest was in Wisconsin ($24,650), Utah ($25,840) and Ohio ($26,220). The most jobs were in Texas (5,170), New York (3,190) and California (2,810). The occupation is most concentrated in District of Columbia, where its share of all jobs is 5.8 times the national share.
