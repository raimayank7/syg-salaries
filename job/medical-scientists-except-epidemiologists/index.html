Medical Scientists, Except Epidemiologists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Medical Scientists, Except Epidemiologists in the United States earned a median wage of $103,410 a year ($49.72 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Conduct research dealing with the understanding of human diseases and the improvement of human health. Engage in clinical investigation, research and development, or other related activities. Includes physicians, dentists, pharmacologists, and medical pathologists who primarily conduct research. Practitioners who primarily provide medical or dental care or dispense drugs are included in “Healthcare Diagnosing or Treating Practitioners” (29-1000).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 19-1042.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Medical Scientists, Except Epidemiologists is $103,410, which is 103% higher. Among the 48 occupations of the field Life, Physical, and Social Science, it ranks number 12 by median pay. With 172,340 jobs, it makes up about 11 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($116,840) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Medical Scientists, Except Epidemiologists pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($136,990), New Jersey ($129,280) and Massachusetts ($128,210). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($49,650), Mississippi ($54,040) and North Dakota ($64,470). The most jobs were in California (29,830), Massachusetts (17,100) and Pennsylvania (12,630). The occupation is most concentrated in Massachusetts, where its share of all jobs is 4.2 times the national share.
