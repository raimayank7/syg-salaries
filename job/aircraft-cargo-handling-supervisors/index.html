Aircraft Cargo Handling Supervisors: pay in the United States, May 2025

Aircraft Cargo Handling Supervisors in the United States earned a median wage of $58,170 a year ($27.97 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Supervise and coordinate the activities of ground crew in the loading, unloading, securing, and staging of aircraft cargo or baggage. May determine the quantity and orientation of cargo and compute aircraft center of gravity. May accompany aircraft as member of flight crew and monitor and handle cargo in flight, and assist and brief passengers on safety and emergency procedures. Includes loadmasters.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 53-1041.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Aircraft Cargo Handling Supervisors is $58,170, which is 14% higher. Among the 50 occupations of the field Transportation and Material Moving, it ranks number 22 by median pay. With 9,760 jobs, it makes up about 0.6 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($65,010) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Aircraft Cargo Handling Supervisors pay by state

The highest median pay was in Georgia ($81,380), Ohio ($79,650) and Louisiana ($79,460). The lowest was in Oklahoma ($41,600), Michigan ($47,850) and Puerto Rico ($51,150). The most jobs were in California (1,490), Texas (1,410) and Kentucky (1,010). The occupation is most concentrated in Kentucky, where its share of all jobs is 8.0 times the national share.
