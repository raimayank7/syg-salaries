Insurance Claims and Policy Processing Clerks: pay in the United States, May 2025

Insurance Claims and Policy Processing Clerks in the United States earned a median wage of $49,230 a year ($23.67 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Process new insurance policies, modifications to existing policies, and claims forms. Obtain information from policyholders to verify the accuracy and completeness of information on claims forms, applications and related documents, and company records. Update existing policies and company records to reflect changes requested by policyholders and insurance company representatives. Excludes “Claims Adjusters, Examiners, and Investigators” (13-1031).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 43-9041.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Insurance Claims and Policy Processing Clerks is $49,230, which is 3% lower. Among the 54 occupations of the field Office and Administrative Support, it ranks number 26 by median pay. With 214,260 jobs, it makes up about 14 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Insurance Claims and Policy Processing Clerks pay by state

The highest median pay was in New Hampshire ($60,650), Washington ($60,220) and New Jersey ($59,810). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($30,910), Alabama ($38,140) and New Mexico ($41,700). The most jobs were in Texas (24,470), California (23,470) and Florida (15,920). The occupation is most concentrated in Maine, where its share of all jobs is 1.9 times the national share.
