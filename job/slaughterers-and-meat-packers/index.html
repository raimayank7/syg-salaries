Slaughterers and Meat Packers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Slaughterers and Meat Packers in the United States earned a median wage of $40,130 a year ($19.29 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Perform nonroutine or precision functions involving the preparation of large portions of meat. Work may include specialized slaughtering tasks, cutting standard or premium cuts of meat for marketing, making sausage, or wrapping meats. Work typically occurs in slaughtering, meat packing, or wholesale establishments. Excludes “Meat, Poultry, and Fish Cutters and Trimmers” (51-3022) who perform routine meat cutting.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 51-3023.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Slaughterers and Meat Packers is $40,130, which is 21% lower. Among the 105 occupations of the field Production, it ranks number 87 by median pay. With 69,950 jobs, it makes up about 4.5 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Slaughterers and Meat Packers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Colorado ($50,480), Arizona ($49,020) and Nebraska ($48,910). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($22,710), Nevada ($30,430) and New Mexico ($30,560). The most jobs were in California (6,170), North Carolina (6,120) and Nebraska (5,480). The occupation is most concentrated in Nebraska, where its share of all jobs is 11.9 times the national share.
