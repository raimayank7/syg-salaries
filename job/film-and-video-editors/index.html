Film and Video Editors: pay in the United States, May 2025

Film and Video Editors in the United States earned a median wage of $75,420 a year ($36.26 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Edit moving images on film, video, or other media. May work with a producer or director to organize images for final production. May edit or synchronize soundtracks with images. Excludes “Sound Engineering Technicians” (27-4014).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 27-4032.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Film and Video Editors is $75,420, which is 48% higher. Among the 41 occupations of the field Arts, Design, Entertainment, Sports, and Media, it ranks number 10 by median pay. With 25,610 jobs, it makes up about 1.6 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($86,130) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Film and Video Editors pay by state

The highest median pay was in New York ($103,440), District of Columbia ($98,830) and Massachusetts ($92,130). The lowest was in Alaska ($38,800), South Dakota ($39,090) and Montana ($42,520). The most jobs were in California (8,110), New York (3,710) and Florida (1,490). The occupation is most concentrated in District of Columbia, where its share of all jobs is 3.2 times the national share.
