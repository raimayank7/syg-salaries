Foreign Language and Literature Teachers, Postsecondary: pay in the United States, May 2025

Foreign Language and Literature Teachers, Postsecondary in the United States earned a median wage of $79,350 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Teach languages and literature courses in languages other than English. Includes teachers of American Sign Language (ASL). Includes both teachers primarily engaged in teaching and those who do a combination of teaching and research.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-1124.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Foreign Language and Literature Teachers, Postsecondary is $79,350, which is 56% higher. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 26 by median pay. With 19,830 jobs, it makes up about 1.3 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($89,990) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Foreign Language and Literature Teachers, Postsecondary pay by state

The highest median pay was in New Hampshire ($101,750), Delaware ($101,100) and California ($99,860). The lowest was in Mississippi ($54,670), Oklahoma ($61,580) and Arkansas ($62,900). The most jobs were in California (2,170), New York (1,520) and Texas (1,460). The occupation is most concentrated in Vermont, where its share of all jobs is 4.4 times the national share.
