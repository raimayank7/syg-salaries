Construction Managers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Construction Managers in the United States earned a median wage of $114,990 a year ($55.28 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Plan, direct, or coordinate, usually through subordinate supervisory personnel, activities concerned with the construction and maintenance of structures, facilities, and systems. Participate in the conceptual development of a construction project and oversee its organization, scheduling, budgeting, and implementation. Includes managers in specialized construction fields, such as carpentry or plumbing.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 11-9021.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Construction Managers is $114,990, which is 126% higher. Among the 37 occupations of the field Management, it ranks number 18 by median pay. With 380,360 jobs, it makes up about 24 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Construction Managers pay by state

The highest median pay was in New York ($155,360), Washington ($155,070) and Massachusetts ($145,010). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($77,380), Arkansas ($86,310) and Virgin Islands ($89,950). The most jobs were in Texas (59,060), California (34,750) and Florida (34,010). The occupation is most concentrated in Guam, where its share of all jobs is 2.8 times the national share.
