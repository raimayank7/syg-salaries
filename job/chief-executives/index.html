Chief Executives: pay in the United States, May 2025

Chief Executives in the United States earned a median wage of $213,990 a year ($102.88 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Determine and formulate policies and provide overall direction of companies or private and public sector organizations within guidelines set up by a board of directors or similar governing body. Plan, direct, or coordinate operational activities at the highest level of management with the help of subordinate executives and staff managers.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 11-1011.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Chief Executives is $213,990, which is 320% higher. Among the 37 occupations of the field Management, it ranks number 1 by median pay. With 204,350 jobs, it makes up about 13 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($269,630) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Chief Executives pay by state

The highest median pay was in Oregon ($341,630), Washington ($339,810) and Illinois ($331,920). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($97,510), Virgin Islands ($104,010) and Arkansas ($119,990). The most jobs were in California (34,700), Pennsylvania (8,850) and Texas (8,780). The occupation is most concentrated in Virgin Islands, where its share of all jobs is 3.0 times the national share.
