Gambling Dealers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Gambling Dealers in the United States earned a median wage of $34,320 a year ($16.50 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Operate table games. Stand or sit behind table and operate games of chance by dispensing the appropriate number of cards or blocks to players, or operating other gambling equipment. Distribute winnings or collect players’ money or chips. May compare the house’s hand against players’ hands.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 39-3011.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Gambling Dealers is $34,320, which is 33% lower. Among the 32 occupations of the field Personal Care and Service, it ranks number 28 by median pay. With 83,910 jobs, it makes up about 5.4 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($42,550) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Gambling Dealers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Oregon ($62,230), Wisconsin ($61,670) and Arizona ($60,940). The lowest was in Louisiana ($17,760), Georgia ($21,430) and Montana ($21,940). The most jobs were in Nevada (17,390), California (15,050) and Florida (5,370). The occupation is most concentrated in Nevada, where its share of all jobs is 20.8 times the national share.
