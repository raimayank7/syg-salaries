Postal Service Clerks: pay in the United States, May 2025

Postal Service Clerks in the United States earned a median wage of $62,130 a year ($29.87 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Perform any combination of tasks in a United States Postal Service (USPS) post office, such as receive letters and parcels; sell postage and revenue stamps, postal cards, and stamped envelopes; fill out and sell money orders; place mail in pigeon holes of mail rack or in bags; and examine mail for correct postage. Includes postal service clerks employed by USPS contractors.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 43-5051.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Postal Service Clerks is $62,130, which is 22% higher. Among the 54 occupations of the field Office and Administrative Support, it ranks number 4 by median pay. With 73,720 jobs, it makes up about 4.7 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Postal Service Clerks pay by state

The highest median pay was in Guam ($68,810), District of Columbia ($67,790) and New Jersey ($64,290). The lowest was in North Dakota ($57,820), Alaska ($57,820) and South Dakota ($58,470). The most jobs were in California (6,410), New York (5,030) and Texas (4,900). The occupation is most concentrated in Virgin Islands, where its share of all jobs is 4.2 times the national share.
