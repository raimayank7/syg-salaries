Hearing Aid Specialists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Hearing Aid Specialists in the United States earned a median wage of $65,160 a year ($31.33 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Select and fit hearing aids for customers. Administer and interpret tests of hearing. Assess hearing instrument efficacy. Take ear impressions and prepare, design, and modify ear molds. Excludes “Audiologists” (29-1181).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-2092.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Hearing Aid Specialists is $65,160, which is 28% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 55 by median pay. With 11,270 jobs, it makes up about 0.7 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Hearing Aid Specialists pay by state

The highest median pay was in New York ($82,990), Nevada ($81,800) and California ($80,060). The lowest was in West Virginia ($35,110), Nebraska ($45,660) and Alabama ($46,530). The most jobs were in Florida (1,150), California (990) and Texas (970). The occupation is most concentrated in Idaho, where its share of all jobs is 2.5 times the national share.
