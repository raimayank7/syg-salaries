Electrical Engineers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Electrical Engineers in the United States earned a median wage of $120,630 a year ($58.00 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Research, design, develop, test, or supervise the manufacturing and installation of electrical equipment, components, or systems for commercial, industrial, military, or scientific use. Excludes “Computer Hardware Engineers” (17-2061).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 17-2071.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Electrical Engineers is $120,630, which is 137% higher. Among the 36 occupations of the field Architecture and Engineering, it ranks number 8 by median pay. With 198,750 jobs, it makes up about 13 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Electrical Engineers pay by state

The highest median pay was in New Mexico ($158,520), California ($144,040) and District of Columbia ($143,000). The lowest was in Guam ($64,190), Puerto Rico ($74,250) and Montana ($87,360). The most jobs were in California (24,230), Texas (20,870) and Michigan (10,840). The occupation is most concentrated in Guam, where its share of all jobs is 2.3 times the national share.
