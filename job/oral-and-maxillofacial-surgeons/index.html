Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons: pay in the United States, May 2025

Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons in the United States earned a median wage of $352,220 a year ($169.34 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Perform surgery and related procedures on the hard and soft tissues of the oral and maxillofacial regions to treat diseases, injuries, or defects. May diagnose problems of the oral and maxillofacial regions. May perform surgery to improve function or appearance.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-1022.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons is $352,220, which is 591% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 7 by median pay. With 4,910 jobs, it makes up about 0.3 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons pay by state

The highest median pay was in Pennsylvania ($553,740), Minnesota ($475,250) and Rhode Island ($414,060). The lowest was in Illinois ($70,220), Michigan ($97,040) and Georgia ($129,010). The most jobs were in California (670), New York (350) and Virginia (210). The occupation is most concentrated in Alaska, where its share of all jobs is 4.1 times the national share.
