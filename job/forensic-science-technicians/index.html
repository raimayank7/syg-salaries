Forensic Science Technicians: pay in the United States, May 2025

Forensic Science Technicians in the United States earned a median wage of $72,060 a year ($34.65 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Collect, identify, classify, and analyze physical evidence related to criminal investigations. Perform tests on weapons or substances, such as fiber, hair, and tissue to determine significance to investigation. May testify as expert witnesses on evidence or crime laboratory techniques. May serve as specialists in area of expertise, such as ballistics, fingerprinting, handwriting, or biochemistry.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 19-4092.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Forensic Science Technicians is $72,060, which is 41% higher. Among the 48 occupations of the field Life, Physical, and Social Science, it ranks number 34 by median pay. With 19,120 jobs, it makes up about 1.2 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Forensic Science Technicians pay by state

The highest median pay was in Illinois ($124,790), California ($102,100) and Connecticut ($85,910). The lowest was in Arkansas ($44,670), South Dakota ($51,690) and South Carolina ($51,830). The most jobs were in California (2,800), Florida (1,920) and Texas (1,900). The occupation is most concentrated in Wyoming, where its share of all jobs is 2.5 times the national share.
