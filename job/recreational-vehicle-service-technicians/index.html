Recreational Vehicle Service Technicians: pay in the United States, May 2025

Recreational Vehicle Service Technicians in the United States earned a median wage of $52,000 a year ($25.00 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Diagnose, inspect, adjust, repair, or overhaul recreational vehicles including travel trailers. May specialize in maintaining gas, electrical, hydraulic, plumbing, or chassis/towing systems as well as repairing generators, appliances, and interior components. Includes workers who perform customized van conversions. Excludes “Automotive Service Technicians and Mechanics” (49-3023) and “Bus and Truck Mechanics and Diesel Engine Specialists” (49-3031) who also work on recreation vehicles.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 49-3092.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Recreational Vehicle Service Technicians is $52,000, which is 2% higher. Among the 51 occupations of the field Installation, Maintenance, and Repair, it ranks number 35 by median pay. With 17,430 jobs, it makes up about 1.1 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Recreational Vehicle Service Technicians pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($68,240), Nevada ($65,200) and New Hampshire ($62,090). The lowest was in West Virginia ($35,920), South Dakota ($39,820) and Virginia ($41,280). The most jobs were in Texas (1,890), Indiana (1,290) and California (1,070). The occupation is most concentrated in Idaho, where its share of all jobs is 3.9 times the national share.
