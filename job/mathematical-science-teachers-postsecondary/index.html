Mathematical Science Teachers, Postsecondary: pay in the United States, May 2025

Mathematical Science Teachers, Postsecondary in the United States earned a median wage of $79,940 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Teach courses pertaining to mathematical concepts, statistics, and actuarial science and to the application of original and standardized mathematical techniques in solving specific problems and situations. Includes both teachers primarily engaged in teaching and those who do a combination of teaching and research.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-1022.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Mathematical Science Teachers, Postsecondary is $79,940, which is 57% higher. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 25 by median pay. With 47,670 jobs, it makes up about 3.1 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($91,550) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Mathematical Science Teachers, Postsecondary pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($130,680), Delaware ($101,510) and Michigan ($99,330). The lowest was in Arkansas ($54,340), Florida ($60,700) and Mississippi ($61,760). The most jobs were in Texas (4,870), New York (4,080) and California (3,810). The occupation is most concentrated in Vermont, where its share of all jobs is 1.8 times the national share.
