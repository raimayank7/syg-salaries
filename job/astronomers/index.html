Astronomers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Astronomers in the United States earned a median wage of $128,820 a year ($61.94 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Observe, research, and interpret astronomical phenomena to increase basic knowledge or apply such information to practical problems.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Astronomers is $128,820, which is 153% higher. Among the 48 occupations of the field Life, Physical, and Social Science, it ranks number 4 by median pay. With 2,120 jobs, it makes up about 0.1 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Astronomers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Maryland ($132,620), Arizona ($129,020) and Virginia ($128,930). The lowest was in Hawaii ($102,000), California ($123,540) and Virginia ($128,930). The most jobs were in Maryland (980), California (110) and Arizona (100). The occupation is most concentrated in Maryland, where its share of all jobs is 26.1 times the national share.

How much do Astronomers earn in the United States?

About 2,120 jobs in the United States in May 2025, not counting the self-employed.
