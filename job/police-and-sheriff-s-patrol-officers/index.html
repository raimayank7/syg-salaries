Police and Sheriff's Patrol Officers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Police and Sheriff's Patrol Officers in the United States earned a median wage of $76,210 a year ($36.64 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Maintain order and protect life and property by enforcing local, tribal, state, or federal laws and ordinances. Perform a combination of the following duties: patrol a specific area; direct traffic; issue traffic summonses; investigate accidents; apprehend and arrest suspects, or serve legal processes of courts. Includes police officers working at educational institutions.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 33-3051.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Police and Sheriff's Patrol Officers is $76,210, which is 49% higher. Among the 24 occupations of the field Protective Service, it ranks number 7 by median pay. With 670,520 jobs, it makes up about 43 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Police and Sheriff's Patrol Officers pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($118,880), Washington ($102,080) and Illinois ($100,520). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($45,510), Mississippi ($46,140) and Arkansas ($47,670). The most jobs were in California (65,940), Texas (63,100) and New York (53,470). The occupation is most concentrated in Puerto Rico, where its share of all jobs is 2.9 times the national share.
