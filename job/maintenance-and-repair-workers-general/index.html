Maintenance and Repair Workers, General: pay in the United States, May 2025

Maintenance and Repair Workers, General in the United States earned a median wage of $49,590 a year ($23.84 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Perform work involving the skills of two or more maintenance or craft occupations to keep machines, mechanical equipment, or the structure of a building in repair. Duties may involve pipe fitting; HVAC maintenance; insulating; welding; machining; carpentry; repairing electrical or mechanical equipment; installing, aligning, and balancing new equipment; and repairing buildings, floors, or stairs. Excludes “Facilities Managers” (11-3013) and “Maintenance Workers, Machinery” (49-9043).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 49-9071.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Maintenance and Repair Workers, General is $49,590, which is 3% lower. Among the 51 occupations of the field Installation, Maintenance, and Repair, it ranks number 39 by median pay. With 1,529,700 jobs, it makes up about 98 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Maintenance and Repair Workers, General pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($60,310), Alaska ($60,270) and Minnesota ($59,200). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($23,220), Guam ($30,450) and Virgin Islands ($38,850). The most jobs were in California (143,060), Texas (140,580) and New York (119,650). The occupation is most concentrated in Virgin Islands, where its share of all jobs is 2.2 times the national share.
