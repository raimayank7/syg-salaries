Semiconductor Processing Technicians: pay in the United States, May 2025

Semiconductor Processing Technicians in the United States earned a median wage of $51,430 a year ($24.73 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Perform any or all of the following functions in the manufacture of electronic semiconductors: load semiconductor material into furnace; saw formed ingots into segments; load individual segment into crystal growing chamber and monitor controls; locate crystal axis in ingot using x-ray equipment and saw ingots into wafers; and clean, polish, and load wafers into series of special purpose furnaces, chemical baths, and equipment used to form circuitry and change conductive properties.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 51-9141.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Semiconductor Processing Technicians is $51,430, which is about the same. Among the 105 occupations of the field Production, it ranks number 30 by median pay. With 31,460 jobs, it makes up about 2.0 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($60,180) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Semiconductor Processing Technicians pay by state

The highest median pay was in Arizona ($78,940), Oregon ($63,330) and Florida ($62,330). The lowest was in Nevada ($37,290), Texas ($38,440) and Arkansas ($40,610). The most jobs were in Oregon (6,620), Texas (6,110) and California (4,650). The occupation is most concentrated in Oregon, where its share of all jobs is 16.6 times the national share.
