Title Examiners, Abstractors, and Searchers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Title Examiners, Abstractors, and Searchers in the United States earned a median wage of $58,650 a year ($28.20 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Search real estate records, examine titles, or summarize pertinent legal or insurance documents or details for a variety of purposes. May compile lists of mortgages, contracts, and other instruments pertaining to titles by searching public and private records for law firms, real estate agencies, or title insurance companies. Excludes “Loan Officers” (13-2072).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 23-2093.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Title Examiners, Abstractors, and Searchers is $58,650, which is 15% higher. Among the 8 occupations of the field Legal, it ranks number 8 by median pay. With 48,580 jobs, it makes up about 3.1 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Title Examiners, Abstractors, and Searchers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Colorado ($77,600), West Virginia ($77,230) and Oregon ($76,930). The lowest was in Louisiana ($38,470), Puerto Rico ($44,570) and Indiana ($46,150). The most jobs were in Texas (6,770), Florida (4,800) and Pennsylvania (2,220). The occupation is most concentrated in Oklahoma, where its share of all jobs is 2.8 times the national share.
