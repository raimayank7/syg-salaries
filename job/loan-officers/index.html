Loan Officers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Loan Officers in the United States earned a median wage of $76,690 a year ($36.87 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Evaluate, authorize, or recommend approval of commercial, real estate, or credit loans. Advise borrowers on financial status and payment methods. Includes mortgage loan officers and agents, collection analysts, loan servicing officers, loan underwriters, and payday loan officers.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 13-2072.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Loan Officers is $76,690, which is 50% higher. Among the 32 occupations of the field Business and Financial Operations, it ranks number 23 by median pay. With 274,330 jobs, it makes up about 18 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($87,790) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Loan Officers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Massachusetts ($101,600), Connecticut ($95,730) and New York ($95,710). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($35,730), Virgin Islands ($44,780) and Guam ($45,180). The most jobs were in California (25,790), Texas (21,200) and Florida (18,830). The occupation is most concentrated in South Dakota, where its share of all jobs is 2.3 times the national share.
