Boilermakers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Boilermakers in the United States earned a median wage of $76,410 a year ($36.74 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Construct, assemble, maintain, and repair stationary steam boilers and boiler house auxiliaries. Align structures or plate sections to assemble boiler frame tanks or vats, following blueprints. Work involves use of hand and power tools, plumb bobs, levels, wedges, dogs, or turnbuckles. Assist in testing assembled vessels. Direct cleaning of boilers and boiler furnaces. Inspect and repair boiler fittings, such as safety valves, regulators, automatic-control mechanisms, water columns, and auxiliary machines.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 47-2011.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Boilermakers is $76,410, which is 50% higher. Among the 60 occupations of the field Construction and Extraction, it ranks number 4 by median pay. With 10,190 jobs, it makes up about 0.7 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Boilermakers pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($118,150), Mississippi ($105,910) and New Mexico ($101,200). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($29,120), Maryland ($42,250) and North Carolina ($49,720). The most jobs were in Texas (2,340), Louisiana (1,220) and California (720). The occupation is most concentrated in Louisiana, where its share of all jobs is 9.6 times the national share.
