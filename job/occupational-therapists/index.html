Occupational Therapists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Occupational Therapists in the United States earned a median wage of $100,330 a year ($48.24 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Assess, plan, and organize rehabilitative programs that help build or restore vocational, homemaking, and daily living skills, as well as general independence, to persons with disabilities or developmental delays. Use therapeutic techniques, adapt the individual’s environment, teach skills, and modify specific tasks that present barriers to the individual. Excludes “Rehabilitation Counselors” (21-1015).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-1122.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Occupational Therapists is $100,330, which is 97% higher. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 36 by median pay. With 162,450 jobs, it makes up about 10 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Occupational Therapists pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($124,510), Oregon ($112,310) and Nevada ($110,520). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($51,850), North Dakota ($83,200) and New Hampshire ($83,900). The most jobs were in California (13,810), Texas (13,400) and New York (11,690). The occupation is most concentrated in Connecticut, where its share of all jobs is 1.8 times the national share.
