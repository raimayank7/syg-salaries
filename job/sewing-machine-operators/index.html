Sewing Machine Operators: pay in the United States, May 2025

Sewing Machine Operators in the United States earned a median wage of $36,670 a year ($17.63 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Operate or tend sewing machines to join, reinforce, decorate, or perform related sewing operations in the manufacture of garment or nongarment products.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 51-6031.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Sewing Machine Operators is $36,670, which is 28% lower. Among the 105 occupations of the field Production, it ranks number 101 by median pay. With 104,880 jobs, it makes up about 6.7 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Sewing Machine Operators pay by state

The highest median pay was in Nevada ($45,750), Alaska ($43,850) and Colorado ($43,840). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($21,840), West Virginia ($29,320) and Kentucky ($29,460). The most jobs were in California (13,240), North Carolina (8,290) and Texas (6,950). The occupation is most concentrated in Puerto Rico, where its share of all jobs is 8.6 times the national share.
