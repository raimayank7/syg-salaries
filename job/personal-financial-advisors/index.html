Personal Financial Advisors: pay in the United States, May 2025

Personal Financial Advisors in the United States earned a median wage of $105,070 a year ($50.51 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Advise clients on financial plans using knowledge of tax and investment strategies, securities, insurance, pension plans, and real estate. Duties include assessing clients' assets, liabilities, cash flow, insurance coverage, tax status, and financial objectives. May also buy and sell financial assets for clients. Excludes “Securities, Commodities, and Financial Services Sales Agents” (41-3031).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 13-2052.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Personal Financial Advisors is $105,070, which is 106% higher. Among the 32 occupations of the field Business and Financial Operations, it ranks number 2 by median pay. With 266,800 jobs, it makes up about 17 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($156,670) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Personal Financial Advisors pay by state

The highest median pay was in New York ($166,400), New Jersey ($158,570) and California ($130,330). The lowest was in Mississippi ($63,300), Kentucky ($67,150) and Oklahoma ($68,670). The most jobs were in California (32,200), New York (21,550) and Florida (21,490). The occupation is most concentrated in Kansas, where its share of all jobs is 1.7 times the national share.
