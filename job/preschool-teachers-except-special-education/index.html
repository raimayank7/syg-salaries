Preschool Teachers, Except Special Education: pay in the United States, May 2025

Preschool Teachers, Except Special Education in the United States earned a median wage of $38,140 a year ($18.34 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Instruct preschool-aged students, following curricula or lesson plans, in activities designed to promote social, physical, and intellectual growth. Excludes “Special Education Teachers” (25-2050), “Substitute Teachers, Short-Term” (25-3031), and “Childcare Workers” (39-9011).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-2011.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Preschool Teachers, Except Special Education is $38,140, which is 25% lower. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 63 by median pay. With 478,780 jobs, it makes up about 31 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($43,030) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Preschool Teachers, Except Special Education pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($62,150), Vermont ($49,730) and Hawaii ($48,780). The lowest was in Guam ($22,120), Alabama ($28,310) and Kentucky ($29,780). The most jobs were in California (55,700), Texas (41,200) and Florida (35,740). The occupation is most concentrated in Massachusetts, where its share of all jobs is 1.7 times the national share.
