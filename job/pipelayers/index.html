Pipelayers: pay in the United States, May 2025

Pipelayers in the United States earned a median wage of $49,000 a year ($23.56 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Lay pipe for storm or sanitation sewers, drains, and water mains. Perform any combination of the following tasks: grade trenches or culverts, position pipe, or seal joints. Excludes “Welders, Cutters, Solderers, and Brazers” (51-4121).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 47-2151.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Pipelayers is $49,000, which is 4% lower. Among the 60 occupations of the field Construction and Extraction, it ranks number 48 by median pay. With 33,050 jobs, it makes up about 2.1 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($54,700) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Pipelayers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Wisconsin ($86,870), Washington ($82,450) and Minnesota ($81,980). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($25,620), Arkansas ($37,650) and West Virginia ($40,440). The most jobs were in Texas (5,010), Florida (4,050) and North Carolina (3,830). The occupation is most concentrated in North Carolina, where its share of all jobs is 3.6 times the national share.
