Security Guards: pay in the United States, May 2025

Security Guards in the United States earned a median wage of $38,020 a year ($18.28 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Guard, patrol, or monitor premises to prevent theft, violence, or infractions of rules. May operate x-ray and metal detector equipment. Excludes “Police Officers” (33-3050) and “Transportation Security Screeners” (33-9093).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 33-9032.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Security Guards is $38,020, which is 25% lower. Among the 24 occupations of the field Protective Service, it ranks number 22 by median pay. With 1,283,470 jobs, it makes up about 83 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($42,470) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Security Guards pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($64,210), Alaska ($47,480) and Washington ($47,100). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($21,840), Guam ($22,090) and West Virginia ($28,800). The most jobs were in California (199,480), New York (134,700) and Texas (106,210). The occupation is most concentrated in Virgin Islands, where its share of all jobs is 3.9 times the national share.
