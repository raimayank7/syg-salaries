Special Effects Artists and Animators: pay in the United States, May 2025

Special Effects Artists and Animators in the United States earned a median wage of $102,030 a year ($49.06 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Create special effects or animations using film, video, computers, or other electronic tools and media for use in products, such as computer games, movies, music videos, and commercials.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 27-1014.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Special Effects Artists and Animators is $102,030, which is 100% higher. Among the 41 occupations of the field Arts, Design, Entertainment, Sports, and Media, it ranks number 2 by median pay. With 19,970 jobs, it makes up about 1.3 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($112,870) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Special Effects Artists and Animators pay by state

The highest median pay was in Washington ($133,480), California ($130,540) and Nevada ($97,350). The lowest was in Iowa ($47,530), Nebraska ($50,830) and Kentucky ($50,970). The most jobs were in California (7,410), New York (1,540) and Florida (1,530). The occupation is most concentrated in Oregon, where its share of all jobs is 3.4 times the national share.
