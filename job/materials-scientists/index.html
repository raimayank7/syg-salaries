Materials Scientists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Materials Scientists in the United States earned a median wage of $117,790 a year ($56.63 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Research and study the structures and chemical properties of various natural and synthetic or composite materials, including metals, alloys, rubber, ceramics, semiconductors, polymers, and glass. Determine ways to strengthen or combine materials or develop new materials with new or specific properties for use in a variety of products and applications. Includes glass scientists, ceramic scientists, metallurgical scientists, and polymer scientists.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 19-2032.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Materials Scientists is $117,790, which is 131% higher. Among the 48 occupations of the field Life, Physical, and Social Science, it ranks number 8 by median pay. With 8,470 jobs, it makes up about 0.5 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Materials Scientists pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($177,030), Oregon ($150,550) and Wisconsin ($137,700). The lowest was in Tennessee ($69,010), Montana ($71,810) and Texas ($72,700). The most jobs were in California (1,030), Massachusetts (890) and New York (530). The occupation is most concentrated in Massachusetts, where its share of all jobs is 4.5 times the national share.
