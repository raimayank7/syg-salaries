Atmospheric, Earth, Marine, and Space Sciences Teachers, Postsecondary: pay in the United States, May 2025

Atmospheric, Earth, Marine, and Space Sciences Teachers, Postsecondary in the United States earned a median wage of $103,170 a year in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Teach courses in the physical sciences, except chemistry and physics. Includes both teachers primarily engaged in teaching, and those who do a combination of teaching and research.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-1051.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Atmospheric, Earth, Marine, and Space Sciences Teachers, Postsecondary is $103,170, which is 102% higher. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 5 by median pay. With 9,900 jobs, it makes up about 0.6 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($116,020) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Atmospheric, Earth, Marine, and Space Sciences Teachers, Postsecondary pay by state

The highest median pay was in California ($133,230), New Jersey ($131,930) and New Hampshire ($130,230). The lowest was in South Dakota ($63,160), South Carolina ($76,500) and Mississippi ($77,670). The most jobs were in New York (1,730), California (1,410) and Texas (690). The occupation is most concentrated in Maine, where its share of all jobs is 3.1 times the national share.
