Museum Technicians and Conservators: pay in the United States, May 2025

Museum Technicians and Conservators in the United States earned a median wage of $51,440 a year ($24.73 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Restore, maintain, or prepare objects in museum collections for storage, research, or exhibit. May work with specimens such as fossils, skeletal parts, or botanicals; or artifacts, textiles, or art. May identify and record objects or install and arrange them in exhibits. Includes book or document conservators.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 25-4013.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Museum Technicians and Conservators is $51,440, which is about the same. Among the 64 occupations of the field Educational Instruction and Library, it ranks number 56 by median pay. With 12,310 jobs, it makes up about 0.8 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($57,410) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Museum Technicians and Conservators pay by state

The highest median pay was in Maryland ($76,900), District of Columbia ($76,110) and New York ($74,550). The lowest was in Louisiana ($32,350), Utah ($35,430) and Delaware ($38,080). The most jobs were in California (1,610), New York (1,050) and Missouri (850). The occupation is most concentrated in District of Columbia, where its share of all jobs is 9.3 times the national share.
