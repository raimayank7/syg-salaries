First-Line Supervisors of Mechanics, Installers, and Repairers: pay in the United States, May 2025

First-Line Supervisors of Mechanics, Installers, and Repairers in the United States earned a median wage of $79,860 a year ($38.39 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Directly supervise and coordinate the activities of mechanics, installers, and repairers. May also advise customers on recommended services. Excludes team or work leaders.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for First-Line Supervisors of Mechanics, Installers, and Repairers is $79,860, which is 57% higher. Among the 51 occupations of the field Installation, Maintenance, and Repair, it ranks number 7 by median pay. With 617,500 jobs, it makes up about 40 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

First-Line Supervisors of Mechanics, Installers, and Repairers pay by state

The highest median pay was in Alaska ($98,110), Washington ($94,420) and Connecticut ($94,050). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($47,660), Guam ($48,760) and Virgin Islands ($58,660). The most jobs were in Texas (75,340), California (51,670) and Florida (41,250). The occupation is most concentrated in Alaska, where its share of all jobs is 2.1 times the national share.
