Anthropologists and Archeologists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Anthropologists and Archeologists in the United States earned a median wage of $70,770 a year ($34.02 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Study the origin, development, and behavior of human beings. May study the way of life, language, or physical characteristics of people in various parts of the world. May engage in systematic recovery and examination of material evidence, such as tools or pottery remaining from past human cultures, in order to determine the history, customs, and living habits of earlier civilizations.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 19-3091.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Anthropologists and Archeologists is $70,770, which is 39% higher. Among the 48 occupations of the field Life, Physical, and Social Science, it ranks number 35 by median pay. With 8,990 jobs, it makes up about 0.6 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Anthropologists and Archeologists pay by state

The highest median pay was in Massachusetts ($121,300), Nebraska ($94,520) and Connecticut ($91,420). The lowest was in Kansas ($41,870), Louisiana ($47,750) and Maryland ($54,160). The most jobs were in California (2,060), Florida (620) and Arizona (500). The occupation is most concentrated in Alaska, where its share of all jobs is 6.7 times the national share.
