Order Clerks: pay in the United States, May 2025

Order Clerks in the United States earned a median wage of $46,170 a year ($22.20 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Receive and process incoming orders for materials, merchandise, classified ads, or services such as repairs, installations, or rental of facilities. Generally receives orders via mail, phone, fax, or other electronic means. Duties include informing customers of receipt, prices, shipping dates, and delays; preparing contracts; and handling complaints. Excludes “Dispatchers, Except Police, Fire, and Ambulance” (43-5032) who both dispatch and take orders for services.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 43-4151.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Order Clerks is $46,170, which is 9% lower. Among the 54 occupations of the field Office and Administrative Support, it ranks number 35 by median pay. With 75,200 jobs, it makes up about 4.8 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Order Clerks pay by state

The highest median pay was in Massachusetts ($55,040), Oregon ($50,260) and Vermont ($49,950). The lowest was in Louisiana ($37,520), Alaska ($37,590) and Tennessee ($39,160). The most jobs were in California (12,390), Texas (5,180) and Washington (4,300). The occupation is most concentrated in Oklahoma, where its share of all jobs is 2.7 times the national share.
