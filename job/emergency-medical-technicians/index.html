Emergency Medical Technicians: pay in the United States, May 2025

Emergency Medical Technicians in the United States earned a median wage of $44,470 a year ($21.38 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Assess injuries and illnesses and administer basic emergency medical care. May transport injured or sick persons to medical facilities. Excludes “Paramedics” (29-2043), “Firefighters” (33-2011), and “Ambulance Drivers and Attendants, Except Emergency Medical Technicians” (53-3011).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 29-2042.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Emergency Medical Technicians is $44,470, which is 13% lower. Among the 71 occupations of the field Healthcare Practitioners and Technical, it ranks number 70 by median pay. With 180,510 jobs, it makes up about 12 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Emergency Medical Technicians pay by state

The highest median pay was in Hawaii ($66,410), Alaska ($57,440) and New Jersey ($53,610). The lowest was in Puerto Rico ($27,610), Oklahoma ($34,170) and West Virginia ($34,360). The most jobs were in California (21,880), New York (14,840) and Texas (12,770). The occupation is most concentrated in Vermont, where its share of all jobs is 2.2 times the national share.
