Political Scientists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Political Scientists in the United States earned a median wage of $142,080 a year ($68.31 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Study the origin, development, and operation of political systems. May study topics, such as public opinion, political decisionmaking, and ideology. May analyze the structure and operation of governments, as well as various political entities. May conduct public opinion surveys, analyze election results, or analyze public documents. Excludes “Survey Researchers” (19-3022).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 19-3094.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Political Scientists is $142,080, which is 179% higher. Among the 48 occupations of the field Life, Physical, and Social Science, it ranks number 3 by median pay. With 5,540 jobs, it makes up about 0.4 of every 10,000 jobs in the country.

Political Scientists pay by state

The highest median pay was in Maryland ($153,380), District of Columbia ($152,530) and Washington ($147,060). The lowest was in New Hampshire ($74,560), Michigan ($86,290) and Illinois ($103,780). The most jobs were in District of Columbia (3,070), Michigan (180) and Massachusetts (170). The occupation is most concentrated in District of Columbia, where its share of all jobs is 122.8 times the national share.
