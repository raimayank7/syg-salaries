News Analysts, Reporters, and Journalists: pay in the United States, May 2025

News Analysts, Reporters, and Journalists in the United States earned a median wage of $62,200 a year ($29.91 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Narrate or write news stories, reviews, or commentary for print, broadcast, or other communications media such as newspapers, magazines, radio, or television. May collect and analyze information through interview, investigation, or observation.

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 27-3023.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for News Analysts, Reporters, and Journalists is $62,200, which is 22% higher. Among the 41 occupations of the field Arts, Design, Entertainment, Sports, and Media, it ranks number 25 by median pay. With 39,250 jobs, it makes up about 2.5 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($102,460) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

News Analysts, Reporters, and Journalists pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($105,390), New York ($100,770) and Georgia ($81,910). The lowest was in West Virginia ($35,280), Wyoming ($36,040) and Arkansas ($37,450). The most jobs were in New York (5,750), California (4,140) and Texas (2,670). The occupation is most concentrated in District of Columbia, where its share of all jobs is 9.7 times the national share.
