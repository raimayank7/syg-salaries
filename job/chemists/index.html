Chemists: pay in the United States, May 2025

Chemists in the United States earned a median wage of $91,240 a year ($43.87 an hour) in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Half of them earned more than that and half earned less.

What the occupation covers

Conduct qualitative and quantitative chemical analyses or experiments in laboratories for quality or process control or to develop new products or knowledge. Excludes “Biochemists and Biophysicists” (19-1021) and “Geoscientists, Except Hydrologists and Geographers” (19-2042).

Official definition from the Standard Occupational Classification, code 19-2031.

How it compares

The median for all occupations in the United States was $50,980 a year. The median for Chemists is $91,240, which is 79% higher. Among the 48 occupations of the field Life, Physical, and Social Science, it ranks number 22 by median pay. With 82,770 jobs, it makes up about 5.3 of every 10,000 jobs in the country. The average ($100,450) is well above the median, a sign that a smaller group of high earners pulls the average up.

Chemists pay by state

The highest median pay was in District of Columbia ($149,860), Maryland ($140,670) and Delaware ($129,860). The lowest was in Montana ($61,890), Maine ($64,440) and South Dakota ($67,440). The most jobs were in California (9,820), Pennsylvania (6,800) and New Jersey (5,280). The occupation is most concentrated in Delaware, where its share of all jobs is 4.7 times the national share.
