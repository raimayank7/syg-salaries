Tax on A$120,000 in Australia, 2026-27

For a resident with taxable income of A$120,000 and the full tax-free threshold, income tax in 2026-27 is A$26,520 and the Medicare levy at 2% is A$2,400, together A$28,920. That leaves A$91,080 before offsets.

The working

  1. Nil on the first A$18,200.
  2. 15c on A$26,800 (A$18,201 to A$45,000): A$4,020
  3. 30c on A$75,000 (A$45,001 to A$120,000): A$22,500

Income tax: A$26,520. Medicare levy: 2% of A$120,000 = A$2,400.

Compared with 2025-26

In 2025-26 the same income paid A$26,788 of income tax, which is A$268 more than in 2026-27, because the rate in the second bracket is 16c then and 15c now.

What the numbers mean

The marginal rate, the rate on the next dollar you earn, is 30% income tax plus the 2% Medicare levy, which is 32% in all. The average rate, tax and levy divided by income, is 24.1%. An extra A$1,000 of taxable income would cost about A$320 in tax and levy, so you would keep about A$680 of it. The average is lower than the marginal rate because the first A$18,200 is taxed at nil and each bracket only applies to the slice of income inside it.

Not included

  • Offsets such as the low income tax offset, which can reduce the tax at lower incomes.
  • HELP repayments, the Medicare levy reduction or surcharge.
  • Super, which the employer pays on top of the salary.
