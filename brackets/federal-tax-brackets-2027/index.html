How brackets work

Brackets are marginal: only the part of taxable income inside a bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate. Taxable income is income after the standard deduction ($16,600 for a single filer in 2027) or itemized deductions and other adjustments. The IRS table also shows the tax on the amount below each bracket, which saves adding up the lower brackets.

Which table is mine?

Use the single table if you are unmarried and not a head of household. Married couples choose joint or separate returns: the joint thresholds are twice the single ones up to the 32% bracket in these tables, which is why filing jointly often costs less. A head of household has wider lower brackets than a single filer. Check the IRS definition of each status before choosing.
