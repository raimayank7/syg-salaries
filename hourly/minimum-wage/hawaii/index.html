Minimum wage in Hawaii: $16.00 an hour

The minimum wage in Hawaii is $16.00 an hour, as listed by the U.S. Department of Labor on 5 November 2026. That is $8.75 more than the federal minimum wage of $7.25.

How it compares

At $16.00, the state rate is 121% above the federal minimum of $7.25. Over a 2,080-hour year that difference is $18,200 before tax. 34 of the 50 states and territories with a rate in the table are above the federal minimum; Hawaii is number 6 from the top. The median hourly wage of all employees in Hawaii was $27.08 in May 2025 (Bureau of Labor Statistics): half earned more, half less. The $16.00 minimum is 59% of that median.

What the Department of Labor's table says about Hawaii

  • Basic Minimum Rate (per hour): $16.00
  • Premium Pay After Designated Hours: Weekly - 40
  • An employee earning a guaranteed monthly compensation of $4,000 or more is exempt from the State minimum wage and overtime law.
  • Domestic service workers are subject to Hawaii's minimum wage and overtime requirements. Act 248, Regular Session 2013.
  • The State law excludes from coverage any employment that is subject to the federal Fair Labor Standards Act unless the State wage rate is higher than the federal rate.

Quoted from the Department's page as updated on 5 November 2026. The overtime premium rate is one and one-half times the employee's regular rate, unless otherwise specified.

Before you rely on a rate

  • Cities and counties in some states set their own, higher minimum wage. This page shows the state entry only.
  • Workers who receive tips, young workers, trainees and some small employers can come under different rates or exemptions.
  • Where a state rate and the federal rate both apply, the worker is entitled to the higher one.
  • Rates are usually changed on 1 January or 1 July. Check the date at the top of this page.
